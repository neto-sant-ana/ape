# Observation 7 — Reaching each other does not join two lines

Convergence concluded that Source and Target are roles rather than sides, and that the asymmetry is
a **gain** — a transfer in the other direction is a different question with a different right answer,
and symmetry would have been a loss. That conclusion stands. With two parties it has a price, and
this is the first arrangement able to see it.

## The measurement

Both parties converge, then each takes up the other's plan:

```text
one party    equipping ──▶ staffing      a world holding both plans, on its branch
other party  staffing  ──▶ equipping     a world holding both plans, on its branch
```

Two worlds. They agree about everything a world is made of except one thing:

```text
open           identical      { budget, hiring, equipment }
frozen         identical      { }
known_at       identical      2026-01-10
thesis_parent  differs        staffing  ·  equipping
```

And neither descends from the other, so they are two tips with no relation between them. No later
decision can prefer one by extending it, because extending one is *not* a statement about the other.

## What that costs, stated as a cost

The two parties have agreed. Nothing in the repository says so.

The record holds two worlds that select exactly the same commitments and no way to express that
they are one plan reached twice. A reader looking for *what are we doing* finds two answers that
happen to coincide, and the coincidence is only visible by comparing selections — which is derived,
so nothing was written down wrong. There is simply nothing to write.

> Two parties reaching each other produces agreement about content and no record of agreement.

This is the same shape as provenance's central finding, arriving from the other end. There, the
missing thing was *where an intention came from*, and it turned out to have no consumer. Here the
missing thing is *that two lines converged on one plan*, and there is now a second party who would
be the consumer.

## Not a defect, and this is why it is recorded rather than repaired

Neither world is wrong. A fork of `staffing` is a statement about `staffing`, and a party whose line
is `staffing` is right to make it — the two worlds differ precisely because the two parties were
reasoning from different places, which is what having two parties means.

And the alternative is worse in a way this experiment can already name. A merge that produced **one**
world from a mutual adoption would have to pick a parent, which means picking a party — the
repository would settle, silently, whose branch the shared plan belongs on. Two worlds is the honest
answer to a question with two answers.

What is missing is not a joined world. It is a way to say *these two are the same plan*, which is a
statement about the relation between two lines rather than about either of them.

Which is exactly the shape of the thing the previous experiment handed forward and this one's second
half is about. Recorded here, as a cost measured before Part B rather than an argument for it: the
gap is real, and whether a decider record fills it is a separate question that Phase 5 answers on its
own terms.

## And the case that looks the same and is not

Two parties adopting in the **same** direction produce one world and one decision. Measured by
mutating the arrangement to do exactly that, which dropped the lineage from five decisions to four.

```text
opposite directions   two worlds, same selection, different parents
same direction        one world, one record
```

So it is not that a transfer produces a new world per party. Identity is derived from content, and
the content of an adoption includes which line it was taken into.
