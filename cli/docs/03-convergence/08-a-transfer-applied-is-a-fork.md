# Observation 8 — A transfer applied is a fork, and the record cannot say otherwise

The applicable transfer was carried into its Target as a decision, and everything about the
result is ordinary.

```text
decides    fork
extends    the Target
omitted    { }              ← the transfer's remove
introduced { inventory, maintenance }   ← the transfer's introduce
after      the last entry admitted
witness    the prefix it stood on
```

No field was added. The closed set Phase 4 pinned is unchanged, the world is a child of its
Target at its Target's cut, and a fresh process reproduces it through the same path as the six
before it. The hypothesis's third claim holds: **a transfer is a decision, recorded as one and
reproduced as one.**

The report and the world agree, which is worth checking rather than assuming: the candidate
Synthesis described and the selection the fork produced are the same set. A report that predicted
a world nobody can build would be evidence about nothing.

And the verdict it carries is the reason to have applied anything. Each line was inside the
account's bounds alone; holding both is not.

```text
the inventory line   60 − 40 − 15  =   5    no conflict
the equipment line   60 − 30 − 25  =   5    no conflict
both                                = −50    OutOfBounds
```

Synthesis said the transfer was applicable and Synthesis was right. Applicability is about what a
Target may hold — freezing, anachronism, dependency closure — and whether the account can carry
the result is a different judgement with a different owner. The two do not overlap, and neither
is wrong for not answering the other.

## What the record stopped being able to say

The decision names what was introduced. It does not name the Base, the Source, or that another
line of thinking is why. So the question Phase 4 deferred comes back with something to be asked
about: is that a loss?

Counted rather than argued. Given only the repository, how many transfers produce exactly this
world?

```text
(inventory line → equipment line)      the transfer that was taken
(applied world  → advanced)            the result, into what the line had advanced to
(applied world  → equipment line)      the result, into its own Target
```

**Three.** So the applied world does not identify the transfer that produced it, and no amount
of searching will make it: recovering provenance by looking is not available even in an
arrangement this small.

The count was written down as two before the run and the measurement corrected it. The one
missed reaches the same selection through a different Target, which is precisely the kind of
thing a guess does not find and a search does.

## What that settles, and what it does not

It settles the experiment's question. Reproduction never needed the Base: the world comes back
whole, by identity, through the ordinary path. A repository that records the choice and not the
computation loses nothing a reconstruction wants.

It does not settle provenance, and the two should not be confused. Recording the Base and Source
alongside a fork would not change one bit of the world produced — it would change what the record
can *say about why*, and this experiment asked what must be preserved for a world to come back,
not what must be preserved for a reader to understand it.

Two of the three explanations above are degenerate: their Source is the applied world itself, so
they are the answer read backwards rather than a rival account of how it was reached. A reader
who could rule those out would be left with one. Whether that rule is sound in general — whether
a lineage can always exclude a transfer whose Source descends from its Target — is a question
this subject raises and does not answer, and it belongs to whichever experiment takes provenance
as its own subject.
