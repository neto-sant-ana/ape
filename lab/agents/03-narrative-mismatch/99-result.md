# Result

```text
Confirmed, and the boundary is not where it was expected
```

Narrative and record do not conflict. They pass each other.

The checkable claims in both narratives are almost entirely true, and almost entirely
inessential to why either narrative convinces. The weight sits in a register the graph cannot
reach, and nothing in the prose marks the transition.

---

## Against the pre-registration

```text
H1  nothing will be contradicted        refuted, once, in narrative A
H2  persuasion will claim diligence     held, and arrived without persuasion too
H3  constraint will be narrated as judgment  refuted
```

**H1 was refuted by the reporting narrative**, which claimed that only one of two candidates
was selected by the world the house ended in. Both are: cancelling the refused one settled it,
and what a cut settles it makes unavoidable. The claim reaches for the engine's division between
knowledge and world and gets wrong which side its own cancellation put the candidate on.

So a competent agent does assert a checkable falsehood — not about arithmetic, which it
computes, but about what an operation *means*. Every number in both documents is right. The one
error is semantic.

**H2 held, and predicted the wrong source.** It expected persuasion to claim diligence.
Narrative A was not persuading anyone and claimed it anyway — *I checked that these verdicts are
live by moving the numbers* — a claim that is true, whose evidence is this experiment's
transcript rather than the graph.

**H3 was refuted, and in the more interesting direction.** Asked to justify, under a frame that
presupposed the decisions were right, narrative B did not dress constraint as judgment. It
accepted fault, named a process failure of its own, and offered four remedies with the choice
left to the owner.

Three of three predictions wrong or misaimed, across three experiments now. The pattern is
consistent enough to state: what these runs produce is never the friction anticipated, and the
frictions they do produce are sharper than the ones designed for.

---

## What the experiment found instead

**A correct measurement, drawn one size too large.** Narrative B did not assert that nothing was
forced on the house — it computed it, by checking on every advancing edge which frozen
commitments the parent had not selected, and finding none. The measurement is right. The
sentence is larger: it is blind to a commitment already present becoming unrevisable, which is
what settling the withdrawal did, and it cannot speak to authorship at all, because an Event
carries no author.

This is the mismatch worth having found. Not a lie, not a rhetorical flourish — a real
operation, correctly run, offered as warrant for a claim it does not reach. It is more
convincing than an unsupported assertion precisely because there is something behind it, and it
is harder to catch for the same reason.

**The record is outrun rather than contradicted.** Both documents convince through an admission
of fault, a claim of vigilance, and a claim of clean causation. None of the three is settleable.
All the settleable claims are true, and none of them is what carries the reader.

---

## Consequences

**None for the engine.** As in the two experiments before it, nothing here traces to a missing
primitive, field or operation. The registers the graph cannot reach are the ones experiment 02
already established it does not carry, and filling them was argued there to be the weaker
alternative.

**One for anyone building on this.** A graph of this kind is a poor instrument for checking a
justification, and a good instrument for checking a *claim*. The difference is that a
justification is mostly not claims. Sorting narrative A took seven operations and found one
error; sorting narrative B took eight and found none — and the sorting missed the point of both
documents until the unsupported register was written out beside the supported one.

If a record is going to be offered as a check on what an agent says, the useful output is not
*this claim is false*. It is *this claim is outside what I can see* — and nothing in the current
boundary produces that answer, because it requires knowing what was claimed, which is not the
engine's business.

**One for the sequence.** The three experiments are done and the claim they were testing now
reads:

> *An immutable, content-addressed operational knowledge graph makes an agent's decisions
> historically reconstructible, projectable, and auditable in the counterfactual sense — what
> was knowable, what it implied, what alternatives existed — without extending its ontology.*

Every clause is supported by a run. The qualifier on *auditable* was earned in experiment 02 and
is confirmed here from the other side: what the graph cannot audit is exactly where a
justification puts its weight.

Multiple agents and Synthesis were named as what comes next if these three survived. They
survived.
