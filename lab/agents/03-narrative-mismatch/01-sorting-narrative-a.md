# Phase 1 — Narrative A, sorted

Narrative A is `01-single-agent/run-01/ANSWER.md`: a real justification of a real decision,
written by an agent that did not know it would ever be checked.

Every substantive claim is placed in a register below. Claims placed in *contradicted* or
*supported* are settled by running an operation, in `tests/narrative_a.rs`, one test per claim
and named as the claim. Nothing here is settled by assertion.

---

## Contradicted

**"The two candidate commitments coexist in canonical history, and only one of them is selected
by the world the house ends up in."**

The first half holds. The second does not. Cancelling the refused candidate *settled* it, and
what a cut settles it makes unavoidable — so that world selects both: the refused one frozen,
the taken one open.

The sentence is reaching for the engine's division between knowledge and world, and the
division is real. What it gets wrong is which side its own cancellation put the candidate on.
Cancelling did not remove the intention from the world. It froze it there, without a movement.

Settled by `claim_only_one_candidate_is_selected_is_contradicted`, which asserts the
contradiction rather than the claim.

**This refutes H1**, which predicted no claim in either narrative would be overturned. It is
recorded first because a prediction that failed is worth more than six that held.

## Supported

| Claim | Settled by |
| --- | --- |
| The wanted arrangement cannot be carried out; the account holds 100 and spending 120 puts it at −20; every hypothesis agrees | `claim_the_wanted_arrangement_is_refused_under_every_hypothesis` |
| The undertaken arrangement leaves the account at 70, and nothing was found against it | `claim_the_undertaken_arrangement_leaves_seventy_and_conflicts_with_nothing` |
| Cancelling is what removes an intention's movement | `claim_cancelling_is_what_removes_the_movement` |
| The omit-in-a-fork route was available up to the moment the event was admitted | `claim_omitting_was_available_until_the_cancellation_was_admitted` |
| Any penalty above 70 would have sunk the other path too | `claim_a_penalty_above_seventy_would_have_sunk_the_other_path_too` |

The 70 is folded from the engine's own arithmetic through `movement_of`, not copied from the
narrative.

The last one deserves a note. It is a counterfactual about a quantity the world does not carry,
and it is still checkable — the sequence admits a penalty of 71 and the engine reports the
breach. A claim being about something absent does not put it out of reach; it has to be about
something *unrepresentable* for that.

## Supported in substance, unconstructible as offered

**"The money exists only because the cut recognizes the event that made it. A cut taken before
that event would make every spend look out of bounds."**

Two separate problems, and neither makes the sentence false.

The counterfactual cannot be built. The spending was recorded after the settling event, and the
recording watermark refuses knowledge back-dated into an instant already closed. The proof the
narrative offers cannot be instantiated in the world it describes.

And it would not show what it claims if it could. Under `FinalState` an unsettled commitment
contributes its movement too — asserted directly, by opening a world on an unsettled spend and
watching the level move. So what settlement supplies is not the money. It is the *automatic*
selection of what carries it: settlement freezes the opening into every world the cut
recognizes, where otherwise it would merely have to be selected on purpose.

What survives is the substance, and it is checked: the 100 is the movement of a selected
commitment rather than a stored balance.

This is the closest thing found so far to the fourth register the protocol named as its most
interesting failure. It is not one. It is a true claim resting on an illustration that does not
carry it, which is an ordinary defect of exposition rather than a category the sorting cannot
handle.

## Unsupported — the graph says nothing either way

| Claim | What would make it checkable |
| --- | --- |
| "The house **wanted** the priority slot" | Preference has no representation. A Commitment asserts intention, not ranking between intentions. |
| "the priority slot spares a penalty owed to a third party" | The third party is not an admitted Agent and the penalty has no amount. The narrative says this itself. |
| "I cancelled rather than omitted **because** the house did form the intention" | An Event carries an observation, an occurrence and a recording. No reason, no author. |
| "I checked that these verdicts are live rather than decorative, by moving the numbers" | Nothing records that an interpretation was requested. This is the diligence claim of H2 — and it appears in the *reporting* narrative, not only in the persuasive one. |

The last row is the one worth pausing on, and it changes what Phase 3 is looking for.

H2 predicted that persuasion would produce claims of diligence. Narrative A was not persuading
anybody — it was reporting, and it made one anyway. The claim is also *true*: the run record
shows the mutations were performed. But the evidence for it is the experiment's transcript, not
the graph, and a reader holding only the graph cannot tell that claim from an invented one.

---

## What the sorting cost

Three of the seven checks failed on first run. One was a real contradiction. The other two were
mistakes in how the check was built — a counterfactual that cannot be constructed, and a
mutation that removed a cancellation while leaving the advancement that recognized it, which
the engine refused.

Both were repaired by changing the check rather than the claim, and both are recorded because
the alternative reading — that the narrative was wrong three times — was available and would
have been wrong.
