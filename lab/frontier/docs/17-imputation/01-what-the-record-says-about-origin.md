# Observation 1 — What the record says about origin today

Three things touch origin, and not one of them is where an intention came from. Read once, as a closed
list, so that what a second relation would add is weighed against something measured.

```text
by             the party that TOOK the decision. Every decision the receiving record holds
               claims nobody — including the two it took from another record, which is what
               a candidate writes

extends        an ancestor in THIS lineage. The retaken fork names the world this record
               produced for the one the original extended, so the only ancestry written down
               is its own

worlds.json    the three worlds this record's own decisions produced, and nothing else. The
               source's first world is named nowhere in it
```

The arrangement:

```text
origin     18 entries, 2 decisions, both claiming `analyst`
authored   18 entries, 2 decisions, both claiming `analyst`
relayed    18 entries, 2 decisions — shown origin's material, authored none of it
here       16 entries, 1 decision, claiming nobody
  having   20 entries, 3 decisions: its own, and two of `authored`'s retaken
   taken
```

The answers, all five distinct, so a phase reporting one has said which world:

```text
here                   370 / 370
authored               340 / 340   and   340 / 320
here having taken      310 / 310   and   310 / 290
```

## What that list already settles

**The record has one field a party goes in**, and Phase 3 measures what it is weighed against. There is
no second place: everything else a `Taken` writes is an identity the record resolves — a coordinate, a
prefix, an ancestor — and every one of them is about this record's own history.

So the question is not where a relation would be stored. It is whether the record can hold the
sentence at all, which is what the two shapes in Phase 1 and Phase 2 are for.
