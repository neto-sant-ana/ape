# Imputation

## Abstract

Experiment 15 measured the shape of a decision crossing between two records, and found the place its
origin would go already occupied.

```text
`by` is the party that TOOK the decision. A retaken decision was taken here.

So the receiving record CAN write the other record's party — an AgentId is content-addressed,
it learned the agent along with everything else, and `attributed` accepts it, because it
checks only that the agent was known at the coordinate.

And it would be false.
```

That is where 12's queued item — *recording that a decision was carried rather than taken* — now
stands. It is not asking for a place to put something. It is asking for a **second relation**, whose
subject is the intention rather than the decision, and 15's measurement is that the record has room for
one word and needs two.

This asks whether the second word is one a record may say.

Chosen by measuring vocabulary load first: `attribution` appears 40 times and is what `lineage.rs`
already calls the `by` field's business; `derivation` appears 109. Both are the question rather than a
handle for it. **Imputation** is 0, and it carries the connotation the finding needs — ascribing an act
to somebody, with the possibility that the ascription is not one anybody can settle.

The predictions are lettered **P**, because `I` belongs to indexicality.

---

## Question

**May a decision say where its intention came from — and is that a claim the record can hold, given
that its subject is another record's past?**

The second clause is the experiment, and it is not the same question experiment 13 answered. 13 measured
that a *travelling* decision's reference cannot be checked. This asks about something narrower and
stranger: a claim that is not about knowledge at all, that names something the receiving record has no
way to resolve, and that would be **true** — which is the one thing nothing in this row has yet had to
weigh.

---

## What *came from* means here, decided before any phase runs

The word to settle is **came from**, and settling it is most of the work, because the two candidate
readings are answered differently by the record.

> **A decision's intention came from a source when the source is nameable by something the receiving
> record holds, and naming it asserts nothing the receiving record cannot state truthfully.**

Two shapes the source could take, and they are not interchangeable:

```text
a party      an AgentId. Content-addressed, and the receiver learns the agent when it learns
             everything else, so the name RESOLVES here

a world      a ThesisId. Produced rather than admitted — a world is not in the journal, and
             the record's one way of naming one is `extends`, which refuses a world the
             archive does not hold
```

Three weaker things *came from* does not mean:

```text
who decided       `by`, which 15 measured means the party that TOOK it. Filling it with the
                  source is the state this experiment exists to avoid rather than to build

what it extends   `extends` names an ancestor in THIS lineage. A retaken decision's ancestor
                  is this record's own, which 15 measured and is carried forward

what it agrees    experiment 09's world-level agreement. Two records agreeing about a world
with             says nothing about either being the reason for the other
```

---

## Hypothesis

```text
the record can hold it       one of the two shapes resolves against what a receiver already
                             has, so a second relation is expressible with nothing invented

and it cannot check it       what a record checks it derives, and it derives nothing about a
                             record it does not hold. So a provenance relation is a third
                             value nothing weighs, beside the recording instant and the decider
```

If both hold, the item 12 queued is buildable and its price is a third unverifiable claim. If the first
fails, the item is asking for something that has no name here, and the reason is worth more than the
field.

---

## Pre-registered predictions

Five, written before anything runs.

**P1 — naming the source *party* resolves against what the receiving record already holds.**

*Prediction:* an `AgentId` is content-addressed, and a record that took another's material learned the
agent as one of the entries it learned. So the name is not dangling: the record can look it up and find
an agent it admitted.

*Refuted if* it cannot, or if resolving it needs anything the receiver does not hold.

**P2 — and naming the source *world* does not.**

*Prediction:* a world is produced and never admitted, so it is in no journal and reaches no archive but
the one that produced it. A decision naming a foreign world names something this record cannot resolve,
and the only guard that resolves a world by name refuses exactly that.

*Refuted if* a foreign world is nameable in a way the record can resolve.

**P3 — and neither is checkable.**

*Prediction:* resolving is not checking. That the agent exists here says nothing about whether that
agent's record is where this intention came from, and nothing the receiver derives bears on it.

*Refuted if* some part of it is weighed against something the receiver computes.

**P4 — so it is a third value nothing weighs, beside the recording instant and the decider.**

*Prediction:* stated as the class rather than as the instance, so it can be wrong as a class.
Experiment 13 measured the first, experiment 05 the second, and 14 measured that they are one kind.
This predicts the third joins them rather than forming a new kind.

*Refuted if* it is unverifiable for a different reason than those two are.

**P5 — and it is the first of the three that would be *true*.**

*Prediction:* the interesting one. A recording instant and a decider are values the writer supplies and
nothing corroborates. A provenance relation is a statement about an event that actually happened —
this record was shown that record's material — and the writer is the one party in a position to know it.
So the question stops being *can it be checked* and becomes *what is a record for*.

*Refuted if* the claim turns out to be one the writer is not in a position to know either.

**P3 with P5 is the experiment.** P3 says the claim cannot be settled by the record; P5 says it is
nonetheless the kind of thing a record could be for. Either alone is half: an uncheckable falsehood is
easy to refuse, and an uncheckable truth is the case this row has not met.

## What is carried forward, and is not available as a finding

* **`by` is the party that took the decision, and a retaken one was taken here** — 15.
* **The receiving record can write the other's party and nothing refuses** — 15.
* **A retaken decision's `extends` names this record's own world** — 15.
* **What a record checks, it derives; what it derives, it holds** — 13.
* **A recording instant and a decider are derived from nothing and no record weighs them** — 05, 13,
  and 14 measured that they are one kind.
* **Synthesis translates rather than carries, and the provenance does not survive into the decision** —
  `cli/src/transfer.rs`, earned by 03. *That it does not survive is not a finding. Whether it should is
  the question.*

Before any sentence here is allowed to be a finding, ask what would have to be false for it to be
false.

## Motivation

It goes now because it is the second of two remaining faces of the question the agents row is parked
on, and because experiment 15 changed it from a request into a specific one.

**By ripeness** it has two arrivals, 12 and 15, and the second is the reason the first is answerable:
12 asked for a way to say a decision was carried, and 15 measured that the field it would occupy means
something else and that the record accepts the lie. An item whose shape moved under measurement is
riper than its count.

**By what it costs if deferred** it is an addition to `Taken`, which is the type every subject in both
rows writes. That was the argument for running 13 and 14 first and it is the same argument here, with
the difference that both of those came back refused — so this is the last item on the board that would
change the type at all.

## Experimental Boundary

This experiment asks whether a decision may name the source of its intention.

It includes:

* what each of the two shapes resolves against, measured against a record that took material;
* what any of it is weighed against, as a closed list;
* whether the claim is one the writer is in a position to know, which is a question about the
  arrangement rather than about the code;
* the three guards, over every state;
* what an application would need, and none of it built.

It deliberately excludes:

* **the engine.** A relation between two application-level records is not an ontology question, and
  the test for whether it were one is in `core/src/docs/01-ontology.md`;
* **authenticity.** Whether a claim could be *signed* is the candidate 13 reached the boundary of, and
  reaching it again here is a finding rather than a licence;
* **changing what `by` means.** 15 measured what it means; this asks for a second relation beside it,
  and a phase that redefined the first would be answering an easier question;
* **more than two records.** Excluded throughout the row.

## Experimental Subject

**A subject of this experiment's own**, and the smallest one this row has needed.

What it must express:

```text
a record that took           because a source that was never a source is not the case. The
another's material           arrangement has to have actually crossed something

a source party the           an agent the receiver admitted only because it learned it, so
receiver holds only          P1 is about resolution and not about the base vocabulary
because it learned it

a source world the           the world the original decision produced, which the receiver
receiver does not hold       never produced, so P2 has something to fail against

a decision the receiver      so that a relation naming a source can be told from one naming
took on its own              nothing, and the phase can measure both
```

The second is where the subject could make P1 true for the wrong reason. If the source party were part
of the shared base, it would resolve because both records always had it, and the phase would be
measuring the base rather than the learning.

## Procedure

### Phase 0 — What the record says about origin today

Read once: `by`, `extends`, and the worlds file, and what each is about. Every answer as a literal, so
that what a second relation adds is weighed against something measured.

### Phase 1 — Naming the source party

P1. A relation carrying the source's `AgentId`, put to the receiving record, and what resolving it
actually consults.

### Phase 2 — Naming the source world

P2. The same with a `ThesisId` the receiver never produced, and what refuses it.

### Phase 3 — What any of it is weighed against

P3 and P4, as a closed list over what the receiving record derives.

### Phase 4 — Whether the writer is in a position to know

P5. Not a question about code: it asks what happened in the arrangement, and whether the party writing
the relation was present for it. The phase states what it would take for the writer to be wrong.

### Phase 5 — The three guards

Every state to the coordinate, the witness and the worlds file, and to the reading.

### Phase 6 — What an application would need, and build none of it

Requests, verbatim, in the vocabulary of the need.

### Phase 7 — Part B, if it was earned

A relation whose shape is decided by Phases 0 to 5 and named nowhere above.

## The condition on building it

This adds a field to `Taken`, which is what experiments 13 and 14 both refused to do, each for its own
reason. So the condition inherits both and adds the row's rule for additions:

> **A capability several results asked for is still a want.**

Part B is refused unless the phases show all three:

> **The claim is one the writer is in a position to know**, or the field would hold a guess;
> **the record admits a state a reader is misled by without it** — not merely fails to offer something
> two results wanted; and
> **the second relation does not make the first ambiguous**, because a decision that says both who took
> it and where it came from has two words where readers are used to one.

The third is this experiment's own, and it is the one most likely to be waved past.

---

## Success Criteria

1. *Came from* is defined before any phase runs, and the three weaker readings are each produced.
2. The two shapes are measured separately, and the one that resolves is shown to resolve **because the
   receiver learned it** rather than because it always had it.
3. *Resolves* and *checks* are kept apart, and the phase that measures the first says so.
4. What the claim is weighed against is a closed list, and the answer *nothing* is reported as an
   answer.
5. P5 is answered by what the arrangement did, not by what the field would say.
6. Every literal is written before the run.
7. Nothing is added to the APE engine.
8. Part B is built only against all three halves of the condition, and its absence is a reportable
   result.
9. The sixteen earlier conclusions stand, or the change is recorded as a result of this one.
   Experiment 13's premise is the one at risk, and P3 is how.

**Criteria 3 and 5 are the experiment.**

## Failure Conditions

The severe one:

> If some part of a provenance relation turns out to be checkable by the receiving record, then
> experiment 13's premise — *what a record checks, it derives* — is wrong for a third time in a row it
> has been treated as settled, and every result that leaned on it is listed.

Its neighbour, which is this experiment's own:

> If the writer turns out **not** to be in a position to know, then the relation is a guess and the item
> 12 queued is asking for a field to hold one. That would close the item rather than answer it, and it
> is the outcome the third condition above exists to make visible.

And the ordinary ones:

* a source party that resolves because the base always held it;
* *resolves* reported where what is meant is *checks*, which is the whole distinction;
* the claim's truth argued from the arrangement being honest, when the arrangement was built to be;
* a Part B built because two results wanted it;
* reaching authenticity's boundary and treating it as permission, which is what 13 named and refused.

## Variables Deliberately Left Open

### Whether an unverifiable truth is worth recording

The question underneath, and this experiment measures its terms rather than deciding it. A record full
of true things nobody can check is a different kind of record from one that holds only what it derives.

### What a signature would add

Authenticity's, and reaching its boundary again is not a licence to answer it.

### Whether the source should be a record rather than a party

A third shape, and it is left open because the record has no name for a record. That absence may itself
be the finding.

### Cost

Deferred by seventeen experiments.

## Methodological Constraint

> *Do not introduce an abstraction intended to solve an experiment that has not yet been performed.*

The previous experiments' conclusions are not revised here; where this one finds something that would
have changed them, it is recorded as a finding of this experiment, against the implementation as it
then stood.

And the rules the last ten experiments earned, which apply without amendment. Before recording anything
as a finding, ask what would have to be false for it to be false. Every literal is written before the
run, and a wrong prediction is corrected in the open rather than adjusted. A prediction's justification
must quantify over as much as its claim does. A friction is evidence of a want, not of a need. A phase
must not be satisfied by an arrangement it could not have failed. A negative result must name the space
it searched. A capability several results asked for is still a want. A guard whose two halves read the
same derived value must assert the mutation before it reads. And a measurement taken with an unchecked
instrument is a false conclusion wearing the clothes of evidence.

## Expected Pressure Points

### *Resolves* will be allowed to sound like *checks*

They are one letter apart in a sentence and a world apart in what they buy. A name that resolves proves
the receiver holds an agent by that identity; it proves nothing about the sentence the relation makes.
Experiment 13 spent a whole result on this distinction in another place, and it will have to be made
again here.

### The claim being true will be read as the claim being safe

P5 is the prediction most worth being right about and the most dangerous to hold loosely. A true
unverifiable claim is still unverifiable, and a record that starts holding them is a record whose
guarantees a reader has to sort through rather than rely on.

### The arrangement was built honest, so the writer will look honest

Of course the writer knows: the laboratory made it so. The phase has to ask what it would take for the
writer to be **wrong**, and that question is not answered by the arrangement.

### Two results asked for it, and this is the last field-changing item on the board

Both facts push the same way and neither is evidence. The condition above is written before the phases
for that reason, and the third of its three halves is the one that will be argued past if any is.

## Experimental Principle

Reconstruction asked whether meaning survives.

Divergence asked whether deliberation survives.

Corroboration asked whether the record can be trusted without being believed.

Convergence asked whether two lines of deliberation can still reach each other.

Provenance asked whether a record that can be rebuilt perfectly is thereby understood.

Coordination asked whether a record built for one mind can hold two.

Exploration asked what a record owes the worlds nobody chose.

Atomicity asked who the record's promises are to.

Contention asked whether a promise made to one writer is a promise at all when there are two.

Collision asked whether two records that have never met were already the same record.

Witness asked whether a decision is about everything it happened to come after.

Veracity asked whether a record that agrees with itself has thereby told the truth.

Commensurability asked whether two records that disagree have a common measure anyway.

Indexicality asked whether a decision can say where it stood to somebody who was not standing there.

Individuation asked whether two records that learned the same thing on different days learned one thing.

Assimilation asked whether a record can take what another knows without claiming to have known it.

Custody asked whether a record's word about its own past reaches all of it.

```text
This one asks whether a record may say something
true about somebody else.
```

Every unverifiable value this row has found was a value the writer supplied and nothing corroborates — a
recording instant, a decider. Each of them could be wrong by carelessness. This one could not: the party
writing it was there when the material crossed, and is the only party in a position to know. If the
record refuses it anyway, then what a record holds is not *what is true* but *what it can defend*, and
that is a sentence about this whole system rather than about a field.
