# Observation 4 — An author and a relay are one record, and so is somebody who never met anybody

**P5 is refuted**, by its own refutation criterion, and it is the experiment.

P5 said the writer is the one party in a position to know where the intention came from — that unlike a
recording instant and a decider, this claim could not be wrong by carelessness. The phase asks what it
would take for the writer to be **wrong**, because the arrangement was built honest and therefore
cannot answer whether this one is.

## Two comparisons, and both are of the four files a reader is handed

Read off the disk rather than compared as values, because two records being *equal* is a question about
a `PartialEq` somebody derived and this one is about what a reader gets.

```text
  authored  vs  relayed, claiming the source     IDENTICAL, all four files
  authored  vs  relayed, claiming nobody         lineage.json alone, and only in `by`
  having taken  vs  a record that never met      IDENTICAL, all four files
                    anybody
```

**The first row.** A record that authored the four entries and both intentions, and one that was shown
them by a third record and authored none of it, are the same record — provided the relay writes the
source's party into `by`, which is the sentence experiment 15 measured is accepted and false.

**The second row.** A relay that claims nobody, which is the honest thing and what a candidate writes,
differs in exactly the field that already means something else. And an absent `by` is the ordinary case
of a record reasoning alone, which is the cost 15 recorded for that field being optional: a reader
cannot tell *not this party's* from *unclaimed*.

> So the record has one lever for saying anything about origin. Used truthfully it leaves a relay
> indistinguishable from a record that reasoned alone; used the other way it leaves a relay
> indistinguishable from the author. There is no third state.

**The third row is the one that decides Part B's second condition.** A record that admitted the same
four entries itself and took the same three decisions — built along a path with no crossing and no
translation in it — is byte-identical to the record that took them from another. That is experiment
09's question asked of intentions rather than of knowledge, and the answer is the same.

Seen red: recording the four on another day turns all four files different, and the message names
which.

## Why that refutes P5 rather than qualifying it

The writer was there when the material crossed **into** it. That is one hop.

The relay above holds `analyst`'s party and did not get the intention from `analyst` — it got it from a
record that had it from `analyst`. Measured: a receiver shown the relay and a receiver shown the author
produce **one record**, and both parties resolve in it, and so does a party that was never a source at
all.

```text
the writer is in a position to know      who showed it
the relation as named reads as           where it came from
```

Those are different sentences, and the word this experiment settled before the phases ran does not
distinguish them:

> *A decision's intention came from a source when the source is nameable by something the receiving
> record holds, and naming it asserts nothing the receiving record cannot state truthfully.*

Nameable, yes. Truthfully — of *whom*? The definition has a seam in it that the phases found, and a
seam in the settled word is a finding rather than a nuisance. **The narrower sentence is one the writer
can state truthfully. The one the relation names is not, and the writer has no way to tell which it is
saying.**

So the protocol's closing sentence does not get to be said. It was:

> *If the record refuses it anyway, then what a record holds is not what is true but what it can
> defend.*

The claim was not established as true. What this row has found is not a true thing the record refuses;
it is a thing the writer believed it knew and does not.
