# Observation 5 — Part B refused, and what the phases asked for instead

The condition was written before the phases ran, and it has three halves. Two fail and the third is met
in a form that argues against rather than for.

## The claim is one the writer is in a position to know — **fails**

The writer knows one hop. An author and a relay are the same record, so the party a receiver would name
may be the source or may be somebody the source got it from, and nothing in either record distinguishes
them. A field named for the sentence the item asked for would hold a guess about origin; a field named
for the sentence the writer can state would be a different field, and it is not the one two results
asked for.

## The record admits a state a reader is misled by — **met, and it argues the other way**

There is one, and it is `by` holding the source's party: measured byte-identical to the author, so a
reader is told *this party took this decision* and it did not. That state exists **today** and a second
relation does not remove it. `by` stays writable with a false value; what a second field adds is an
honest alternative, which is a want.

And the state a reader might be *misled* by — a record that took and does not say so — turns out to be
**identical** to a record that reasoned alone. The reader is uninformed rather than misled, and would
be equally uninformed of a record that had nothing to tell.

> A relation cannot remove an ambiguity between two records that are the same record.

## The second relation does not make the first ambiguous — **fails, and on its own terms**

The condition anticipated an ambiguity between two fields. The phases found one **inside** the second
field, before it meets the first: *came from* does not distinguish an origin from a relay, and nothing
the record holds can. Two of the accepted claims in Observation 3's table are false and the record
takes all three.

Adding it to `by`'s existing seam — an absent value already covering *reasoned alone*, *took and claims
nobody*, and *declines to say* — is a second question, and it does not need answering to refuse this.

## What the phases asked for, in the vocabulary of the need, and none of it built

**A name for a record.** The record names a party and a world and has no name for a record. Both shapes
measured here fail for that reason and not for two: a party is too coarse, because a party can relay;
a world is unreachable, because it is derived and never admitted. The protocol left *whether the source
should be a record rather than a party* open as a third shape; the phases found it is not a third
option beside the two, it is what the two were reaching for.

**Something that individuates a crossing.** Experiment 14 asked for something that individuates a
*prefix* rather than an entry. This asks for something that individuates the event of being shown —
which is the thing the writer actually witnessed and the only thing it is in a position to name. The
record has no representation of a crossing at all: it has the material afterwards, and the material
afterwards is what a record reasoning alone also has.

**And the older request is unchanged and now has a reason.** Experiment 12 asked for a way to record
that a decision was carried rather than taken. It cannot be met by naming who it came from, because the
name does not distinguish an author from a relay; and it cannot be met by a flag, because a record that
took and a record that never met anybody are byte-identical, so a flag would be the only thing
separating them and it would be unverifiable on its own.
