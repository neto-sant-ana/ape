## The answer

The question was *may a decision say where its intention came from — and is that a claim the record can
hold, given that its subject is another record's past?*

It may name a party and not a world. And the claim is not one the record can hold, for a reason nobody
on the board had predicted:

> **The writer is in a position to know who showed it, and the relation reads as where it came from.**
> An author and a relay are the same record — measured, byte for byte, in all four files. So the party
> a receiver would name may be the source or may be somebody the source had it from, and no record
> anywhere distinguishes them. The one party in a position to know turns out to be in a position to
> know a **narrower sentence**, and to have no way of telling which of the two it is saying.

That refutes P5 by its own criterion, and with it the protocol's closing line. It read: *if the record
refuses it anyway, then what a record holds is not what is true but what it can defend.* The claim was
never established as true. What this row has found is not a true thing the record refuses — it is a
thing the writer believed it knew and does not.

## What the measurement had that the prediction did not

**A record that took and a record that never met anybody are byte-identical.** Experiment 09's question
asked of intentions rather than of knowledge, built along a path with no crossing and no translation
anywhere in it. It is what settles whether a reader of a record that took is misled or merely
uninformed, and the answer is uninformed — so there is no state a relation would repair.

**The record does check something, and it is a real sentence.** `attributed` is coordinate-relative, so
*you cannot have got this from somebody you had not met yet* is refused by name. Two of the three
accepted claims in that table are false and the record takes all three. The checkable half is the
derivable half, which is **experiment 13's premise standing exactly where 13 put it** — the severe
failure condition does not fire.

**And the class the three unverifiable values belong to is better stated than it was.** Not *nothing
weighs them*, which is what 14 left: **resolution is weighed and the sentence is not.** A recording
instant's ordering is weighed and the instant is not; a decider's resolution is weighed and the
attribution is not; this would be the third of that shape. The weighed half is the half a reader might
mistake for the whole.

## Part B refused, on two of three

Observation 5 has the three halves. The writer is in a position to know a narrower sentence than the
relation names; the misleading state the record admits is one a second relation does not remove, and
the state a reader would be misled *by* is identical to an honest one; and the second relation is
ambiguous **inside itself**, between an origin and a relay, before it ever meets `by`.

Two results asked for this and it was the last item on the board that would change what a `Taken` is.
Both facts push the same way and the protocol said before the phases that neither is evidence.

## Against each criterion

1. ***Came from* is defined before any phase runs**, and the three weaker readings are each produced —
   `by` measured to mean the party that took it, `extends` measured to name this record's own world,
   and world-level agreement inherited from 09. **The definition itself turned out to have a seam**,
   between an origin and a relay, and that is recorded as a finding rather than repaired after the
   fact.
2. **The two shapes are measured separately**, and the one that resolves is shown to resolve because
   the receiver learned it — with a party of the base as the control that would have made it true for
   the wrong reason.
3. ***Resolves* and *checks* are kept apart**, and the phase that measures the first says so — reached
   from the side nobody looks at: a record that learned everything and took **no** intention resolves
   the party just as well.
4. **What the claim is weighed against is a closed list** over all four files a whole write puts on
   disk, and the answer *nothing but the file it is written in* is reported as an answer.
5. **P5 is answered by what the arrangement did** — a relay holding what an author holds — and not by
   what a field would say.
6. **Every literal was written before the run**, and every one held. One refusal was not predicted and
   is recorded as the finding it is: `attributed` refuses the source's party on a decision taken before
   the crossing.
7. **Nothing was added to the APE engine**, and nothing to the application either.
8. **Part B was weighed against all three halves and refused**, and its absence is Observation 5.
9. **The sixteen earlier conclusions stand.** Experiment 13's premise was the one at risk and P3 was
   how; measured, the check is derived from the journal prefix and the premise is unmoved. Experiment
   14's statement that the unverifiable values are one kind stands and is sharpened rather than
   contradicted.

## What this leaves for the row

**A name for a record**, which is the gap both measured shapes were reaching for. A party is too coarse
because a party can relay; a world is unreachable because it is derived and never admitted. The
protocol listed *whether the source should be a record rather than a party* as a third shape left open;
the phases found it is not a third option beside the two.

**Something that individuates a crossing** — the event the writer actually witnessed, and the only
thing it is in a position to name. The record has no representation of one: it has the material
afterwards, and the material afterwards is what a record reasoning alone also has.

And experiment 12's item is **closed rather than answered**. Recording that a decision was carried
cannot be done by naming where it came from, because the name does not distinguish an author from a
relay; and it cannot be done by a flag, because the two records a flag would separate are the same four
files.
