# Observation 7 — What an application would need

Phase 6. Each request is written in the vocabulary of the need rather than of a solution, names the
measurement that produced it, and says what ignoring it costs. **None of them is built.**

They are requests of the **application's record**. Not one of them asks the kernel for anything, which
is the result stated in the negative and is worth putting first.

---

## 1. To know whether I already hold this, before I admit it

> *Before I admit a proposition, I need to know whether it is one I already hold — because afterwards
> I cannot name either occurrence unambiguously, and a decision that names it resolves to the earlier
> one.*

**Measured:** Observation 4. A readmission with anything distinct learned between the two occurrences
produces a repository that is written successfully and cannot be read. Sixteen records addressing
fifteen entries reconstruct; seventeen addressing sixteen are refused.

**Ignoring it costs:** the exact mitigation exploration reaches for first. E4 predicts an application
will revisit ground rather than enumerate fresh ground, and that is the route into this.

**Not asked for:** deduplication in the journal. That would make the journal stop being a faithful log
of what was supplied, and the trade is measured here rather than made.

---

## 2. To record that I weighed a world without claiming I intend it

> *I need to say that I considered a world, in a way a later reader can tell apart from my saying I
> mean it.*

**Measured:** Observation 6. A weighed candidate and a genuine intention are mentioned in the same four
places, and the record has exactly two places that speak about a commitment's relation to a world —
both meaning *intended*. So a repository that explored twelve candidates answers *which propositions
were ever intended* with fourteen where one is.

**Ignoring it costs:** the record's answer is not merely incomplete, it is wrong. An incomplete answer
invites another question; a wrong one does not.

**Flagged honestly:** this is the request nearest the severe failure condition, which names `Trial`,
`Scratch` and `Ephemeral` as the shapes not to grow. The need does not ask for any of them, and the
distinction is why — what is missing lives in the **decision**, not in the proposition. A commitment
weighed and a commitment meant are the same commitment. This experiment reports the need and does not
propose the shape.

---

## 3. To record which of the worlds I weighed I preferred

> *I need a later reader to find the world I chose without re-running the rule I chose it by.*

**Measured:** Observation 6. The candidate the objective chose is mentioned in exactly the places the
eleven it beat are. Nothing records a preference.

**Ignoring it costs:** every reader must hold the objective in order to read the record, and an
objective outside the engine is an objective that can change. Two readers with different objectives
disagree about what a repository says was decided.

**Not a violation of the derived-value rule:** a preference is not derived from the record. It is a
claim, in the same sense a decider is — which the coordination experiment settled the terms for.

---

## 4. To say that something was dropped, or to accept that nothing can

> *When I drop worlds nothing points at, I need the record to be able to say that a drop happened —
> or I need to accept that a pruned repository and one that never explored are the same repository.*

**Measured:** Observation 5. Pruning the leaves and then the admissions nothing witnessed returns all
three files **byte-identical** to the repository nobody explored from.

**Ignoring it costs:** nothing detectable, which is the whole difficulty. This is the only request on
the list whose absence cannot be noticed from inside the record, so it is also the only one where
*accepting* it is a coherent answer rather than a deferral.

**Stated as a fork on purpose.** A tombstone is a solution and this is not the document for one; and
the alternative — deciding that a bounded record simply does not owe the worlds nobody chose anything —
is a legitimate answer to the same need.

---

## 5. To pay for a decision rather than for the journal

> *I need what a decision costs to depend on the decision, not on how much has been admitted by the
> time I take it.*

**Measured:** Observation 3. 260 witnessed entries for thirteen decisions, of which 18,226 of 23,553
lineage bytes are the witness. And 326 for the same thirteen decisions taken later, which is the same
record costing 21% more bytes for having enumerated before judging.

**Ignoring it costs:** the dominant term of the record is driven by something the decision is not
about, and the arrangement an application would reach for — batch, then judge — is the expensive one.

**Not asked for:** removing the witness. Three experiments concluded it is load-bearing, and the last
of them because it is what makes knowledge append-only. What is asked is that the same guarantee be
payable in something that does not grow with the journal.

---

## What none of these asks for

```text
Trial, Discard, Retract, Scratch, Ephemeral, Reward, Score      not requested
a history that can forget                                      not requested
anything at all in kernel/, canon/ or engine/                  not requested
```

The severe failure condition is **not met**. Exploration at this scale asked nothing of the engine and
five things of the record an application keeps around it — and the two nearest to the ontology,
requests 2 and 3, both turn out to be about the **decision** rather than about the proposition, which
is where the coordination experiment already put who decided and the provenance experiment already put
where it came from.

That is the third time the answer has landed in the same place. A decision is where everything that is
not content lives.
