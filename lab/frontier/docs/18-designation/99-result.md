## The answer

The question was *a record's identity is derived from its content, and a reference is a thing that
changes. Can a record say which of its worlds it means — and where does that claim live?*

It can, and it lives in a fifth file. But the shape is not the one any of the three candidates named,
and what forced it is the project's own foundation turned against the remedy:

> **A reference to a content-addressed world cannot be held by anything content-addressed.** The
> journal took the claim and lost the return, because designating `W₁` twice produces one entry
> twice. A bare pointer held the claim and lost the history, because a file with one value has one
> value. What holds a plan is the shape `Taken` already is — a claim *about a world, at a coordinate,
> by somebody* — kept in order, because order is the only thing left that two identical designations
> differ in.

## What the measurement had that the prediction did not

**The failure of A is not a refusal, and that is worse.** P1 expected the Canon to stop it. The Canon
checks references to **admitted** things — a `ResourceInstance` naming an unadmitted `Resource` is
refused, which is the control — and a `ThesisId` is derived and never admitted. So the claim goes in
as free text and nothing between the caller and the file weighs the target. The protocol listed that
state as its fourth failure condition; the experiment produced it rather than avoided it, which is
the only way it could have been reported honestly.

**A plan that returns leaves no trace in what the record claims to hold.** Three moves, and the
journal holds three entries while `custody.json` and every witness hold two. A decision taken
afterwards witnesses twenty of twenty-one and its coordinate addresses the entry where the plan
**first** was. This is `16-custody`'s finding one level over, with the sharper edge: there it was the
tail past the last decision that nothing claimed; here it is an entry *inside* the claimed region
that the claim cannot count twice.

**A supplied fifth file does not survive a whole write.** `custody.json` is derived by the write and
rides inside the all-or-nothing; a file written beside the live generation is left behind by the next
turn, and the record then says it never had a plan. So a designation is an **input to the write** —
`RepositoryInput` grows a field — which is the concrete thing this experiment hands to the
application.

**P4 is confirmed by nothing happening.** Two designations: the second overwrites the first with no
comparison anywhere, and `converge` merges the knowledge and leaves the plan absent. The prediction's
reasoning — a designation is not knowledge, and disagreement about a plan is deliberation — survives
intact and is not what was measured. Recorded as vacuous rather than as confirmed, because the
difference is the whole value of having asked.

**And the two records P5 compares are byte-identical.** A record whose plan went `W₁ → W₂ → W₁` and
one that designated `W₁` and never moved agree in all five files. `17-imputation` ended on this
shape three experiments ago — an author and a relay were one record — and it is now known to be a
shape rather than an incident.

## Against each criterion

1. **A, B and C are each measured, including the ones that fail.** A was attempted rather than
   argued, which was the protocol's first failure condition, and the attempt is what produced the
   result that mattered. C turned out not to be a third home, and the measurement that says so is two
   repositories compared byte for byte.
2. **Additive or refused.** A log is optional on read, so a record with none makes no claim — the
   whole workspace's records read unchanged, and the reason is custody's: four repositories under
   `lab/agents/04-multiagent` were written by parties nobody can re-run.
3. **All five predictions answered, including no.** Two refuted, one confirmed vacuously and said so,
   one split three ways.
4. **What is in the application cites this experiment.** `cli/src/designation.rs`, the fifth field
   on `RepositoryInput`, the comparison in `reading::corroborated` and the union in `converge` each
   carry `Earned by: 18-designation (Confirmed)`, and `cli/tests/pedigree.rs` refuses a citation
   whose result does not reach the verdict it names. The laboratory's prototype is kept beside it,
   and the two are asserted to read each other's bytes.
5. **Nothing added to the engine.** The core delegated `main` in writing; taking it back would have
   answered a different question.

## What the red pass found, and it was in the guard

Mutating `plan_at` to read the log forwards left the guard about *order* green, because `DESIGNATED`
returns to its first world and so the first and last entries name the same one. The property that
makes this arrangement right for Phase 1 is the one thing that hides what Phase 6 measures. The guard
now uses three distinct worlds and asserts that they are distinct.

Recorded because it is the second time in this row that a guard needed the arrangement's own strength
turned off in one place, and because the defect was invisible until a mutation was tried.

## Part B, built after the result was first written

This document was written before the obligation was, which is the reverse of `16-custody`'s order and
is recorded rather than tidied. What the build added is
[Observation 4](04-part-b-and-what-the-build-cost.md) and one phase the protocol did not have:
**Phase 7**, because a whole write takes the log as an input, so `converge` had to supply one and
*what a merge does to two logs* had been measured nowhere. The union keeps both lines and each line's
own order; a claim already present is not added twice; and two **unqualified** claims from two
parties at one coordinate are ordered by arrival, which is a narrow limit and is queued.

The two verdicts above that were about the application's *defects* are the two the build closes. P2's
lifecycle — a fifth file beside the live generation is left behind by the next turn — and P4's
vacuum, where a merge left the record claiming nothing. Both now have a guard through `ape-cli`'s own
path, and all three mutations were made in `cli/src` rather than in a substitute.
