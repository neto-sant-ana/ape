# Observation 5 — The decision, and what it leaves

**Reverted.** The change is gone from `cli/src`, the workspace is green, and the commit it was measured
against is on this branch so the composite column can be reproduced by checking it out.

Three conditions were written before anything ran, and all three fire.

**The trade condition.** *A real buy against a real cost is a trade, and a trade is reported rather
than taken.* The buy is real: the witness completes the pin with no field added, and experiment 13's
misleading state is refused at the coordinate. The cost is real: two records a day apart share nothing,
and the diagnosis experiment 11 built is replaced by a vaguer one.

**The published-result condition.** *A cost that lands on a published result is not a cost this
experiment may pay.* Every one of the seven tests in a concluded agents-row suite fails against four
committed repositories, and those repositories are what that experiment's published result is evidence
for.

**And the boundary.** The experiment excluded the engine, and completing the change requires it — an
address derivable from an identity needs `CanonicalKnowledge` to offer an envelope for seven more
families, which is a port widening and, because conformance requires the instant to survive for two
families only, an obligation every adapter would newly owe. Either of the first two would have been
enough; this one says the change was never fully inside the boundary it was proposed in.

## What a revert is here

Not a failure and not a shelf. It is the answer to the question the protocol asked, and the protocol
pre-registered it as an outcome precisely so that Phase 7 could not be argued out of it by a diff that
was already written and already green.

Which nearly happened, and is worth recording rather than smoothing over. The build compiled on the
first try, cost nine call sites and a docstring, left every one of the application's own tests green,
and confirmed the central prediction. Everything about the moment after the build said *keep it*. What
said otherwise was a table and a compiler error, and neither of them was persuasive on its own.

## What the answer leaves

**The instant does not go at the address, and three results already put it somewhere else.** At a
comparison (11), at an ordering (12), at a decision (13). This is the fourth place and it is refused,
which makes the three earlier ones look less like near-misses than they did.

**The buy is still available and its price is now known.** The witness completes the pin at the stage
the record already writes — that measurement stands, and it is the strongest argument the *decision*
side of the question has ever had. What it costs at the address is what is refused. Whether it can be
bought somewhere else is a question this experiment did not ask, and the shape of it is now specific
enough to ask: something that individuates a **prefix** rather than an **entry**.

That is deliberately not a design. Naming one here is what the methodological constraint forbids, and
the reason it forbids it is visible in this experiment: a shape that looked obviously right cost
something nobody had thought to look for.

**And one sentence in the application stopped being safe to rely on.** `cli/src/lineage.rs` says, about
`Taken.by`, that keeping already-written records readable is *not* a reason to hold a field's shape,
because "a suite that writes and reads with one version of this type is not constrained by it at all".
That was true when it was written and this workspace no longer matches it: four repositories are
written by one version and read by a live suite, and the first change to reach an address is what would
have found out. The note is not wrong about `Taken.by`; it is wrong about the workspace, and the
difference matters to whoever reads it next.
