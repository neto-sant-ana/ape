# Observation 3 — A chain is reached whole, and pruning one link makes another world

An Event's identity contains its predecessor. So a cut that names a head names everything under it, and
the Events before a cut are dependence whether or not any of them settled a commitment the world holds.

That puts a **floor** under the narrowing which has nothing to do with what anybody selected:

```text
what dependence can prune     roles, agents, resources, instances, actions, statements,
                              eligibilities, commitments — anything nothing reaches

what it cannot prune          the Event chain up to the cut, entire
```

## The subject had to be changed to measure it

The first version of this arrangement held one Event, and with one link a closure that reached only the
head is indistinguishable from one that walked. The claim would have been asserted in a comment and
measured nowhere — which is the failure this row of the protocol exists to prevent. A second inflow and a
second Event were added before any of it was recorded, and the base world's selection became `{ F₁, F₂ }`
because both were settled.

Mutating the walk to stop at the head then turns eight guards red, `[13, 16, 16]` against
`[14, 17, 17]`, and the chain test names the cause directly: *the whole chain, not the head*.

## The failure mode is not the one a reader would expect

A record keeping only the head does not come back **missing** an entry. It comes back agreeing with
itself about a **different world**:

```text
journal with both Events        the founding world, as decided
journal with the head only      admits.        decides.        a different ThesisId.
```

Nothing refuses. The shortened journal is well formed, the decision applies, and the world it produces
is coherent — it is simply not the world the record says was decided. What says so is the identity, and
only the identity.

Which is a second reason the Event chain is not prunable, independent of the first: even if one *wanted*
to drop an earlier Event, the successor's identity is a function of it, so dropping it renames the head
and every world whose cut resolved to it. There is no version of that operation that leaves the record
saying what it said.

## Where this lands

It is a **carried-forward premise seen from a new side.** That a cut resolves against the Event chain
that stood is nine experiments old, and the collision experiment measured that one Event's difference
moves a world's identity. What is new is the direction: not *the chain determines the world*, which was
known, but *the world therefore cannot narrow past the chain* — which is a bound on this experiment's
whole subject and was not in the protocol's list of things it expected to find.
