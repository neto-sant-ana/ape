# Observation 5 — Dependence admits all three cases the collision experiment could not

W3, and all three hold. Each was reproduced against the record's own comparison first, so a change in
outcome is attributable to the claim and not to the arrangement.

## The union of two twinned repositories

Two sides, same vocabulary, same three commitments about cash, same three decisions — and one of them
admits four filings no world selects. So they hold the **same worlds by identity** and journals that are
out of step at position 25.

```text
the record's own comparison     LineageError::UnwitnessedKnowledge
                                reproduced, as the collision experiment measured it

by dependence                   admits. three worlds, the identities both sides already
                                held, intending 500, 460 and 430
```

The union journal is one of the two journals — the longer one already holds every entry the other does —
so nothing had to be invented to put them together. What refused was the witness, and only the witness.

## A journal taken without its lineage

The right repository takes the left's journal and keeps its own three decisions. Nothing about its
worlds changes; what changed is what stood.

```text
the record's own comparison     LineageError::UnwitnessedKnowledge
by dependence                   admits. its own lineage, unchanged, over knowledge it
                                did not have when it decided
```

## One decision, one record

The union's lineage held **four records for three worlds**, and the duplicate is the late decision —
the only one taken after the two journals diverged. The other two are already byte-identical on both
sides, which is worth saying plainly: two repositories that decided independently wrote the *same
record* twice, and the deduplication that `converge` already performs found it without being told.

Under dependence the fourth collapses too:

```text
before     4 records, 3 worlds, one of them answered for twice
after      3 records, 3 worlds
```

So the duplicate the collision experiment reported does not have to be lived with. It arose because two
sides claimed different things about knowledge while deciding the same thing about intention, and when
the claim narrows to what the decision is about, the two claims become one claim.

## What each of the three actually needed

None of them needed a new operation, a new file, or anything from the engine. All three needed the
comparison to weigh a different set — which is why the collision experiment was right to fold its three
requests into one candidate, and why that candidate turns on two lines rather than on a design.

That is the *buys* column, complete. Observation 7 is the *sells* column, and it is where the answer
comes from.
