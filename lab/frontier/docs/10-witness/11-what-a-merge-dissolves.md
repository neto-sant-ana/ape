# Observation 11 — What a merge dissolves, and what it only stops checking

This one arrived as a question after the result was written, and it turned out the subject already held
the arrangement to answer it. Recorded here rather than deferred, because it **sharpens the result's own
conclusion** and the sharpening runs against what that conclusion said.

The question: *when two repositories are merged, is the context in which a decision was taken lost?*

## Measured

The right repository's late decision, in the merged record:

```text
entries that stood when it was taken                      23
entries the same coordinate admits in the merged record   29
                                                          ──
knowledge the decider could not have seen                  6

the world it produces                       unchanged, by identity
what the worlds file says                   nothing — it agrees either way
```

So the direction of the question is right. Six entries appear before that decision's coordinate that
were not there when it was taken, and nothing derived from a world notices, because no world is a
function of them.

## And the distinction the measurement forces

The claim is **not destroyed**. It stays a true statement: the twenty-three entries it names still
replay as a history in their own right, and they still produce its world. What it stops being is
**checkable** — because the only thing the record can weigh it against is a prefix of a journal that is
not the one it was taken against.

```text
what a merge destroys        nothing
what a merge dissolves       the record's ability to weigh a claim it still holds
```

That is a smaller loss than *the context is lost*, and a worse one than it sounds. A fact that is true
and unverifiable is exactly the state the veracity candidate is about, arriving from the writing side.

## The crux: the two cases are the same shape

The obvious repair is to weaken the comparison — accept a witness that is a **coherent history**
rather than one that equals the prefix. Three conditions, and they are the most a merged record could
check without being told that more than one line of knowing exists: the claim names entries the journal
holds, it names its own coordinate, and the entries it names replay.

Both of these pass all three:

```text
an imported decision, whose journal was shorter          coherent
a local decision, whose journal GREW UNDERNEATH IT       coherent
```

Measured, and the negative case measured with it — a claim naming an entry no journal here holds is
refused, so the two acceptances are not vacuous.

**A comparison weak enough to admit a merge is exactly weak enough to admit a record that was edited.**
Not approximately: the same shape, and nothing in the record distinguishes them, because nothing in the
record says that there is more than one line of knowing.

## What this does to the result

The result says the three blocked capabilities need *a second comparison, not a different record*. That
is true only if the dissolution is accepted. Stated properly:

> **To merge without dissolving what a decider could have known, the record has to say something it
> does not say: which line of knowing each decision's claim is about.** That is a thing added, not a
> thing removed — and it is the same thing the atomicity experiment asked for as *a witness of the
> journal's extent*, and the same thing the authenticity candidate asks for as *whose record is this*.

So the three positions are these, and none of them is a recommendation:

```text
refuse the merge         what the record does today. Coherent, and stronger than it looks:
                         it is append-only-ness of knowledge stated BETWEEN repositories
                         rather than within one. The cost is that coordination between
                         independent repositories stays read-only forever — two parties can
                         find out they agree and can never act on it

accept the dissolution   defensible only if the record SAYS SO. A repository that quietly
                         stops being able to answer "what could this decider have known" is
                         the shape nine experiments' criterion refuses; one that says which
                         of its decisions arrived from elsewhere is honest

name the line            no dissolution, and a record that holds more. Unmeasured, and the
                         two candidates already queued for other reasons are where it lives
```

## What this experiment did not reach

**An Event arriving into a prefix.** Both sides here hold identical Event chains, so the sharper hazard
is out of range: a coordinate that is *not* an Event, with an Event arriving before it, resolves a
**later head** — which moves the cut and produces a world nobody decided. Observation 3 measured that
such a world comes back coherent rather than broken, and a merge writes its own `worlds.json`, so
nothing would compare it against anything.

That is the first thing an operation for partial meeting has to rule out, and it is not ruled out here.
