# Observation 7 — The witness is the only guard in the record whose subject is history

W4, and the third column is what decides the experiment.

The protocol asked for a closed table of what a narrower claim stops refusing. Building it raised a
question the protocol had not asked, and the question turned out to be the whole answer: *the witness
is not the only guard a mutation has to get past — so which of the others would have caught it anyway?*

A repository holds three files. `lineage::rebuild` weighs the coordinate and the witness against the
journal; `reading::corroborated` then weighs the worlds the decisions **produce** against the worlds the
third file says were decided, and refuses `WorldDisagrees`. Today the witness refuses first and the
worlds file never gets to weigh anything, so the only way to learn what it would have said is to let
each mutation through the narrow comparison and put the result to it.

```text
                             the witness                    by dependence     the worlds file
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
added, not depended on       UnwitnessedKnowledge           silence           silence
removed, not depended on     WitnessedKnowledgeAbsent       silence           silence
removed, DEPENDED on         WitnessedKnowledgeAbsent       DoesNotDecide     (never reached)
a readmission                ReadmittedEntryIsAmbiguous     silence           silence
swapped inside a prefix      silence                        silence           silence
```

Every state the witness refuses and dependence does not, **the worlds file lets through as well.**

That is the finding, and it is a statement about the record's architecture rather than about this
arrangement:

> **Narrowing the witness does not weaken a chain of guards. It removes the only guard whose subject is
> the repository's history rather than its intentions.** The coordinate says which knowledge a world is
> produced against; the worlds file refuses any change that moves a world; between them, a change that
> no world is a function of is invisible. The witness is what is left.

## Why the three silences are one silence

They look like three states and they are one, seen at three angles. Knowledge that came, was not
depended on, and went — added, removed, or admitted twice — changes nothing any world is a function of,
so nothing derived from a world can notice it. That is not a gap in the worlds file; it is what a world
identity *means*, which the collision experiment stated as a rule: a world's identity is blind to
everything but its selection and its cut.

The readmission is the sharpest of the three, because it is the one that costs a **named cause**.
`ReadmittedEntryIsAmbiguous` exists because a witness can outrun a replay for a reason worth naming, and
it was earned by relocating a diagnosis to the layer that held the fact. Under dependence there is
nothing for it to fire on: the claim is a set of entries the world reaches, all of them present and
unambiguous, and the coordinate resolving to an earlier occurrence stops mattering because no comparison
depends on where the coordinate landed. **W5 is refuted**, in the direction the protocol said would cost
something.

## The fifth row, and why it is in the table

Two entries swapped inside a prefix: both silent, and it was already so. A witness is a set, so a
reordering was invisible to it before this experiment existed — which the divergence experiment measured
as *a coordinate that is wrong but well-formed is not detectable from the record*. It is in the table
because leaving it out would have made the narrowing look like the only thing that cannot see a
reordering, and because a table with only the interesting rows is a sample presented as a closed set.

## The one row where dependence refuses

`removed, DEPENDED on` is the row that proves the narrow comparison is a comparison. The entry is gone,
the world cannot be produced, and the refusal names the reason. The two claims are not *strong* and
*weak*; they refuse different things, and both refuse something.

## What follows for the question

The protocol's failure conditions named this outcome in advance:

> If a narrower witness turns out to admit a state a reader cannot tell from a legitimate one, the
> remedy is refused **and the state is reported**.

It does, three times over, and no other guard in the record covers it. So the answer to *can a record
say what a decision depends on, rather than what stood* is **no** — and the reason is not that
dependence is a bad claim. It is that *what I was a function of* and *what was there* are two different
facts, and only one of them is derivable from the worlds.
