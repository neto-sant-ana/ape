## The answer

The question was *does a record's one claim about its own history cover everything the record holds —
or does it stop at the last decision, and is what lies past it lost without consequence?*

It stops, and what lies past it is **not** lost without consequence:

> **A record answered for what it had reasoned about and for nothing else.** The prefix is covered
> because a decision stood on it, not because anything decided the record should answer for it — so an
> entry no world is a function of is covered in the prefix and uncovered in the tail, and the
> difference between those two entries is not what they are. It is which side of the last decision
> they arrived on.

The whole experiment is two rows of one table. A `role` nothing refers to, sitting in the prefix:
removing it is refused, naming the entry. A commitment nothing refers to, sitting in the tail:
removing it is accepted in silence.

## What the measurement had that the prediction did not

**All four combinations of *world moved* and *number moved*.** A gained Event settling a filing
reaches a different world — another chain, another frozen set, another identity — and answers the same
numbers. Which is the protocol's own trap, produced rather than argued: *no answer moved* is not
evidence that nothing was lost, and the two sets come apart in both directions.

**The unreached half is unreached because no Event names it, not because of the resource.** Measured
by admitting one more unsettled outflow on the very resource every world here is about: a later
advance reaches it exactly as much as it reaches a filing. The second resource is doing the other job
— it is what lets a gained Event move a world without moving a number — and reading it as the reason
would have been a claim about the instrument the instrument does not make.

**Custody by reference exists, and is not a claim.** One entry of the tail *is* protected: the Event
names the outflow by identity, so removing the outflow alone leaves a journal that does not replay. It
holds only where something later happens to name the entry, and says nothing about the entry named by
nothing.

**And the quiet half's cost is a decision, not a number.** The record that lost the two filings and
the record that kept them produce the **same later world by identity**. Only one of them can fork it
to introduce a filing; the other is refused with `UnknownCommitment`, which is true and is not the
reason. Second arrival for experiment 13's item about refusals that name the wrong half.

## Part B built, against a condition written before the phases

`custody.json`: a fourth file in the whole write, holding the addresses the journal produces, compared
against the replay on every read. Observation 6 has the four decisions and their reasons; the two that
were not choices are that **the author must be the write** (a decision's authority is what stood when
it was taken) and that **it cannot live in the journal it is about** (a single-file edit would edit the
claim with it).

Seven of the eight states the three guards accepted are now answered, naming the entry on both sides.
The prefix state is still the witness's, which is the right order.

**And it does not strand the four repositories nobody can re-run.** Absent is not empty: a repository
with no `custody.json` is read exactly as it was, and all four of `lab/agents/04-multiagent/run-*/repo`
were measured rebuilding unchanged. That is experiment 14's queued item met for the first time by a
change that could have caused it.

**What it costs**, stated rather than discovered: a whole write now replays the journal it is handed,
every read compares two sets the size of it, and `write_whole` no longer accepts a journal that does
not admit.

## A second finding, and it is not about custody at all

Experiment 14's derived guard — a lexical scan naming every place in `cli/src` an address is compared
— caught `reading::held`, and then **stopped catching it**, ten minutes later, when `rustfmt` fitted
its signature onto one line.

The scan reads a function's **body**. `held` names no address type and no field holding one, because
its addresses arrive as parameters; it was visible only while the type name spilled from a wrapped
signature into what the scan counted as body.

> A guard that depends on where a formatter breaks a line is reading a source adjacent to the one it
> means. It was green, it was derived, and it was wrong — and the only reason anyone knows is that one
> change made it red and then green, in that order.

Repaired here: the declaring line is now scanned too, and the five 14 published are unchanged by the
repair. Recorded rather than absorbed, because 14 **priced a refusal** with that count — the change it
refused costs six sites, not five, and for a stretch of this afternoon a guard that had been offered as
evidence would have said five for a reason unrelated to the code.

It is the second derived guard in this laboratory's corpus to read the wrong source, and the first that
had already been published as evidence when it did.
