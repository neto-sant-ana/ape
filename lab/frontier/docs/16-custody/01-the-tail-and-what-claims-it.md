# Observation 1 — The tail, and what claims it

The arrangement is one record: twenty-six entries, one decision, one world.

```text
journal      26 entries
prefix       22   everything the last decision's witness names
tail          4   an outflow, the Event settling it, and two filings
```

The witness is the prefix **entry for entry**, and disjoint from the tail. Both sets are derived from
the same journal by different routes — the witness is what the record wrote down, the tail is what the
journal offers past the coordinate that witness ends at — so the agreement is a measurement rather
than a restatement.

## What each of the three files says about the difference

```text
journal.json   holds the tail, and says nothing about how much of itself there is
lineage.json   one witness, ending at the coordinate. It names none of the four
worlds.json    names an identity in five places, and none of them is a tail entry
               — it does name what the prefix froze, so the reading is not vacuous
```

## The control, and it is the whole comparison

The prefix holds a `role` labelled `observer` that nothing refers to and no world is a function of.
Measured: no world names it, and a journal without it still admits — which is exactly what is true of
a filing.

So the experiment's central comparison is **two entries of one kind, on two sides of one line**, and
the difference between them is not what they are.

```text
observer   nothing refers to it, no world reaches it, in the prefix   the witness names it
filing     nothing refers to it, no world reaches it, in the tail     nothing names it
```

The witness names the `observer` for one reason only: it stood when the decision was taken. That is
the sentence experiment 10 wrote, read from the other end — a witness is not about what a world
depends on, it is about **what was there**, and the tail was not there yet.
