# Observation 3 — What the next decision stands on

**U3 confirmed, and it is the experiment.** A decision taken after the tail has moved stands on a
different prefix, produces a different world where the tail reached what it selects, and is a
legitimate record either way.

The later decision is an `Advance` to day 20, taken in each record over the journal that record now
holds. Every one of the seven is written whole and read back through the application's own reader.

```text
                                        entries   later world   settled/intended
  nothing moved                            26      W               360/360
  without the whole tail                   22      ≠ W             400/400
  without what nothing reaches             24      W               360/360
  without what something reaches           24      ≠ W             400/400
  with one more nothing reaches            27      W               360/360
  with one more something reaches          27      ≠ W             360/360
  with one more unsettled, on the account  27      W               360/360
```

**All four combinations are in the table**, and the sixth row is the one the protocol was written to
be able to produce: a record that gained an Event settling a filing reaches a **different world** —
another chain, another frozen set, another identity — and answers **the same numbers**, because the
filing moves a resource no world about cash weighs.

> So *no answer moved* is not evidence that nothing was lost, and *an answer moved* is not what a
> claim covers. The two sets come apart in this table, in both directions.

## And a record that lost part of its prefix cannot decide again at all

Which is the difference stated as a capability rather than as a refusal. The covered half of a journal
cannot move without the record noticing, so nothing can be built on top of the move. The uncovered
half can, and what gets built is legitimate.

## Per entry, and the first outcome was not predicted

Each of the four tail entries removed by itself:

```text
  the outflow    the journal does not admit    the Event names a commitment that is gone
  its Event      a different world             the chain a later cut resolves against moves
  filing 5       nothing moved
  filing 7       nothing moved
```

**One entry of the tail is protected after all, and not by any claim.** The Event refers to the
outflow by identity, so removing the outflow alone leaves a journal that does not replay. That is
custody by *reference* — it holds only where something later happens to name the entry, it says
nothing about the entry that is named by nothing, and it is not a claim the record makes.

## What makes the unreached half unreached is the Event, not the resource

Recorded as a finding rather than as a prediction, because reading it the other way would have been a
claim about the instrument that the instrument does not make. The arrangement puts the filings on a
second resource **so that a gained Event can move a world without moving a number** — row six above.
It is not why they are unreached.

Measured: one more unsettled outflow on the very resource every world here is about, admitted past the
coordinate, and the later decision reaches it exactly as much as it reaches a filing. An advance
recognizes what history *settled*; a commitment no Event names is invisible to one whatever it moves.

## But a fork reaches it, and that is where the quiet loss costs something

The record that lost the two filings and the record that kept them produce **the same later world by
identity**. Only one of them can go on to fork it.

```text
  the whole record     Fork introducing filing 5    the world proposes it
  having lost it       the same fork                refused
```

The refusal is `UnknownCommitment`, and it names the commitment. It says nothing about the journal
being short — which is the fact. So the cost of losing the unreached half is not an answer that
changed; it is **a decision the record can no longer take, refused with a reason that is true and is
not the reason**.

That puts it with experiment 13's queued item — refusals that name the wrong half of what a reader
needs — and it is the second arrival for that item rather than a new one.
