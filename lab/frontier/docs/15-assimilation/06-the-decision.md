# Observation 6 — The decision

**Part B refused.** Both halves of the condition were written before the phases and both are answered
against measurements rather than against a preference.

## The first half fails, and it is measured rather than argued

> *The report answers something no existing operation answers — not more conveniently, at all.*

It does not. The harder half of the report is **recomputed inside the test file**, using only
`ape_cli`'s public surface — `reading::corroborated`, `journal::replay`, a set difference — and it
reaches the same ten entries the subject's instrument does.

The crate boundary is what makes that proof rather than illustration. `lab/frontier` depends on
`ape_cli` and the application never depends back, so nothing the laboratory reached for is anything an
application could not reach. The whole of `retaking` is composition: `replay_remaining`,
`lineage::decide`, `Taken::now`. No private door was opened, because there is none to open.

## The second half half-fails

> *It produces a candidate an application could act on without being told anything else.*

For what to learn, yes: ten admissions, dated by the receiver, applied through the ordinary path. For
what to decide, no — Observation 5 measured that the answer is always the whole of the other lineage,
and that this is structural rather than incidental.

Half a candidate does not meet a condition that asks for one.

## And the row's rule aimed at additions

> *A capability several results asked for is still a want.*

Five results asked for this. The protocol said so before anything ran, precisely so that arriving here
with five citations would not read as evidence. It does not read as evidence. What the phases produced
is a **shape**, which is worth more than an operation and is what the row was actually missing.

## What is a defect rather than an absence, and is queued rather than built

One thing measured here is not an absence:

```text
converge, here against there    Diverged { position: 13 }
what was actually takeable      10 entries, and both of their decisions
```

`converge` is the operation an application calls when it is handed another record. It answers with a
position, and a caller reading it concludes the two are incompatible. They are not — ten of the other
record's entries and both of its decisions could have been taken, and nothing says so.

That is a reader being misled, which is the inherited criterion for a repair. It is **not** built here,
and the reason is scope rather than doubt: the protocol's Part B is *an operation whose shape is decided
by Phases 1 to 6*, and a better refusal is not that operation. It joins experiment 13's Request 1 —
refusals that name the wrong half of what a reader needs — as an item, and the two should be answered
together or not at all.

The judgement call is named because it is one. A reader who thinks a misleading refusal outranks a
protocol's scope would build it now, and would not be obviously wrong.
