# Observation 1 — The subject, and what two records can do today

Two records founded apart on a base they admitted independently, and diverged in every family.

```text
base, identical by content                                          12 entries
  F   receive 400   committed day 2, recorded day 2
  E   Event settling F, occurred day 3, recorded day 3

here  adds   Role auditor, Agent third, H receive 150, Eh settling H          16
there adds   Role auditor, Role inspector, Agent third, Agent fourth,
             Eligibility, Resource grain, ResourceInstance silo, Action top-up,
             Statement, G top-up 200, P top-up 90, Eg settling G              24
```

Both admitted `Role auditor` and `Agent third` — the same content, so the same address, which is the
collision experiment's twinning happening **after** the split. So ten of the twelve entries `there`
added are new to `here`, and they cover all nine families.

**That overlap is what keeps the candidate from being an instruction**, and the subject builds the two
records that make it fail as well: one holding everything `there` holds, and one sharing only the base.

## Five worlds, and no two alike

```text
                                        settled  intended
  here decides    { F }                    550      550
  there decides   { F, G }                 600      600
  there forks     introducing P            600      690
  here retakes    the genesis              750      750
  here retakes    the fork                 750      840
```

The retaken pair is higher than either record's own and nothing was added twice: **a genesis absorbs
whatever its cut froze**, so a decision taken here picks up this record's own settled commitment
whether or not the intention named it.

`there` decides twice on purpose. An intention has two shapes and only one of them carries an instant —
a genesis names a `known_at` and a fork names nothing at all, inheriting its parent's cut. Which of the
two survives being retaken elsewhere is the experiment, and a lineage holding only one could not ask.

## What two records can do today

Read before anything was built.

```text
converge        refuses, whole, leaving this record exactly as it was
holds(world)    no — not one world of theirs is one of ours
nothing         takes a single decision
```

And the refusal has a number worth keeping, because it is not the one this observation predicted:

> **`converge` refuses at position 13, which is before the overlap is exhausted.** The two records have
> two entries in common past the base and did not admit them adjacently — `there` put a Role between
> them. Sharing is a question about **membership** and `appended` compares by **position**, so the
> merge stops at the first disagreement and never reaches the second thing they have in common.

Written down as 14 — the size of what they share — and measured as 13. It is the same shape experiment
12 found for the witness and 14 found for the merge: sufficient, and not necessary.

## What the subject could have got wrong

**A divergence of all commitments** would have made the family table report a property of the
arrangement. Every family is present, and the phase counts them from the entries rather than asserting
the number beside them.

**Two records diverging on different days** would have added a second difference, and experiment 14
measured what that costs. Both tails are laid down on the same days; the only difference is *what* was
admitted.

**Two worlds answering alike** would have let a later phase report *the intention crossed* about a world
that was already there. Five worlds, five distinct pairs, asserted before anything runs.
