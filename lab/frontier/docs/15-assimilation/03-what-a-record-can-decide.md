# Observation 3 — What a record can decide with another's intention

**S2 confirmed, and it is the severe one.** Both of `there`'s decisions become decisions `here` takes.
The candidate is written to disk and read back through the application's own reader — which is what
checks a witness against the prefix that actually stood — and the record reads.

```text
readings          its own, then two retaken
retaken worlds    750 / 750  and  750 / 840, which the arrangement named before the run
witness           every entry it names is one this record admitted, and it names all of them
                  and at least one of them is an entry the other record does not hold
```

So the via is **not** experiment 12 with a report in front of it. 12 measured that a *carried* decision
holds a witness that stops being true, and that the two moves available were keeping it (the record
refuses itself) or rewriting it (the record lies). There is a third move and it is the one Synthesis
already makes: do not carry the decision. **Retake it.**

**Seen red by carrying the witness instead.** Substituting the other record's witness into the candidate
turns the phase red with `UnwitnessedKnowledge`, naming an entry — which is 12's first move arriving
exactly where it should. And substituting the other record's *coordinate* is refused earlier still, by
`UnknownEntry`, because the chains diverged and their tip has no address here.

## Two things the intention leaves behind, neither of them predicted

Both were derived while the subject was being built, before anything ran, and are recorded as findings
rather than as predictions.

**A retaken genesis cannot keep the instant the original named.** Measured: retaking at day 9, the day
`there` decided, is refused with `CommitmentNotKnownAtCut`. A record learns a foreign fact when it is
shown it, so the fact is not selectable at any instant before that. The genesis crosses only by being
**re-dated**, and what it then recognizes is not what the original recognized.

**And a fork cannot omit what history froze.** `FrozenPastOmitted`. So the only intention a fork can
carry about a settled past is one that **introduces**, which is why the record being shown has a
commitment it deliberately left open. An intention that was entirely about dropping something already
settled has no shape at all here — not a portability problem, a fork problem, and it belongs to whoever
asks the next question about forks.

Put together they say something the protocol did not: of the two shapes an intention takes, the one
that carries no instant is the one that crosses unchanged, and the one that carries an instant crosses
only by having it replaced. **A fork is portable and a genesis is translatable**, and those are
different words on purpose.
