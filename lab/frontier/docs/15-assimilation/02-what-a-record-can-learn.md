# Observation 2 — What a record can learn, and at whose instant

**S1 confirmed.** The ten entries `here` lacks admit into it, all of them, at day 15 — the day it was
shown them. What the phase reads back is the instant **this** record wrote, not the one it was shown.

That is the whole of what *taking* means, in one measurement: the content crosses and the instant does
not. An entry admitted with the other record's instant would be a copy, and this record would be
claiming a past it did not have.

## And copying is not merely dishonest — it is inadmissible

Found by mutating the instrument rather than by reasoning, which is the only reason it is known. Making
the re-dating a no-op does not produce a dishonest record; it produces no record at all:

```text
RecordedOutOfOrder { recorded_at: 2026-01-04, recorded_through: 2026-01-06 }
```

The Canon refuses. Recording is monotonic across admission and this record's watermark is already past
their days, so the entries cannot go in wearing the instants they arrived with.

**The guarantee is one-sided, and that half must not be overclaimed.** What the Canon forbids is
back-dating, not copying. An instant at or after the receiver's watermark is admissible whoever wrote
it — measured, by re-dating the same entries to this record's own last day and watching them admit. So
a record that is **behind** the one it is shown could copy its instants, and nothing in the engine would
stop it.

Which puts the honesty back where experiment 13 left it: the recording instant is derived from nothing
and no record weighs it. What the Canon protects is the ordering of a history, not the truthfulness of
a claim about when something was learned.

## The two degenerate receivers

Built deliberately, because the phase that reads the real one has to be able to say which of three it
is looking at.

```text
holding everything   lacks 0    it admitted the same content there did
here                 lacks 10   the real one
holding nothing      lacks 12   it shares only the base
```

The middle row is neither, which is the whole of what makes it worth calling a candidate. What that is
worth for the *other* half of a report is Observation 5.
