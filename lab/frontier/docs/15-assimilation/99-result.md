## The answer

The question was *can a record take another's material as its own — learning what it did not know and
deciding what it did not decide — without either record claiming a past that was not its? And is what
that produces a candidate, or an instruction to become the other record?*

It can, and nothing false is written. And the second clause splits:

> **Knowledge is negotiable and intention is not.** What a record has yet to learn is a set that shrinks
> as the two records overlap, and it is the thing a report could usefully say. What a record has yet to
> decide is everything the other decided, always, and no amount of overlap reduces it — because the two
> can share every entry and still not share a single world, since the instants they may claim differ.

## What outlasts the verdict

**A fork is portable and a genesis is translatable.** Of the two shapes an intention takes, the one
carrying no instant crosses unchanged; the one carrying an instant crosses only by having it replaced,
because `ensure_selectable` refuses a commitment recorded after the cut and a learned fact is recorded
today. Derived while the subject was being built, before anything ran, and measured.

**A merge of two sets and a merge of two chains are different operations.** Eight families cross by
identity and the Event does not, and the same Event crosses into a record whose chain never left the
base's. The row is about the chain.

**And *already held* is reachable only by not crossing.** The record that had admitted the same content
and decided at the same instant produced the other's world by identity, with no operation between them.
So two records agree about a world exactly where they agreed about the knowledge **and about when they
held it** — which is experiment 09 with the clause it did not have.

## Part B refused, on both halves of its own condition

## Against each criterion

1. ***Take* is defined before any phase runs**, and the three weaker readings are named apart from it —
   `copy` measured to be inadmissible, `merge` inherited from 12, `agree` measured as the thing that
   needs no operation.
2. **What two records can do today is measured here**, not inherited, and the measurement corrected a
   literal: `converge` refuses at 13, before the overlap is exhausted, because sharing is membership and
   comparison is position.
3. **The witness is checked on the written record**, through the application's own reader, and seen red
   by substituting the other record's.
4. **The family table is closed over all nine**, and the eight predicted not to move are shown not to
   move in both columns.
5. **Both degenerate cases are producible and are named as degenerate**, which is what lets the middle
   row be called a candidate.
6. **Every literal was written before the run.** One was wrong — the position `converge` refuses at —
   and is corrected in the open.
7. **Nothing was added to the APE engine.**
8. **Part B was weighed against both halves and refused**, and its absence is Observation 6.
9. **The fourteen earlier conclusions stand.** Experiment 12's was the one at risk and it is unmoved:
   what it measured is that a *carried* decision cannot be written down, and this measures a third move
   it did not consider rather than contradicting the two it did.

## What this leaves for the row

The row's oldest item — *saying two lines agree*, named by five results — is not closed and is now
shaped. What it wanted was an operation; what it has is the knowledge that the operation is composition,
that the useful half of it is about knowledge rather than intention, and that the half about intention
cannot be made useful because two records cannot arrive at one world by crossing.

Whether an application should offer the composition anyway is a question about convenience, and this
laboratory has no way to measure that.
