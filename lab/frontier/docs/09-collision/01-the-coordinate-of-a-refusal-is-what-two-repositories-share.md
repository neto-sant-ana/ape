# Observation 1 — The coordinate of a refusal is what two repositories share

C1 predicted that a meeting is refused by name and that the **position** of the refusal is the size of
what the two happened to share. Phase 1 measured it, in both directions, and Phase 2 measured the same
number from the other end — off the journals, without running anything.

```text
relation      met(left, right)      met(right, left)      shared prefix, measured
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Disjoint      Diverged at 0         Diverged at 0         0
Shared        Diverged at 13        Diverged at 13        13
Extending     merged               merged                 14
```

Two repositories founded independently diverge at **0** — there is nowhere earlier for the refusal to
point. Two that admitted the same base diverge at the first entry each admitted alone, which is 13,
which is the base. And nothing was written by any refused attempt: both repositories are, by value,
what they were.

## Which turns a refusal into an answer

The refusal was built by the coordination experiment to stop a party writing over a journal that had
moved, and it says so:

> the journal diverges at entry {position}: this party holds {expected}, and {found} is there

Nothing about that sentence was meant to be a measurement of anything. But `position` is the length of
the agreeing prefix, and for two repositories that is the only quantity in the question — so the
operation the application has for *acting* on a meeting turns out to be a usable instrument for
*sizing* one, and it costs a call that changes nothing.

Stated so that it is about the relation rather than about this arrangement:

> **Two repositories are refused a meeting at exactly the point they stop being the same repository.**

## The third relation is not a meeting

`Extending` — one journal that is the other, extended — is not refused. Which is correct and is not a
finding about two repositories: it is the case `converge` was built for, because a journal that extends
is what a party that read late holds. The contention experiment established that the merge accepts
extension and refuses divergence; two repositories are divergent unless one of them has somehow already
got the other's knowledge, and then they are not two repositories meeting, they are one repository and
a reader of it.

So the three relations are two answers and a borrowed case:

```text
disjoint     refused at 0
shared       refused at the base
extending    the case that already worked, wearing the word "meeting"
```

## What it does not say

That the refusal is wrong. It refuses the shape it was built to refuse, measurably, and Observation 3
gives the reason the refusal is *right* — knowledge that no world names is still knowledge a standing
decision was taken against. What is missing is not a weaker comparison. It is an operation whose
subject is two repositories, and Observation 7 measures what the application has instead.
