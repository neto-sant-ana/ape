# Observation 8 — What an application would need, and none of it is built

Phase 6. Requests, verbatim, in the vocabulary of the need rather than of a solution — because naming a
mechanism here would be choosing Part B's answer before the criterion is applied to it. The protocol
excluded four shapes by name; none of them appears below.

---

## Request 1 — *"Put together the part we agree about, and tell me what is left."*

Observations 5 and 7. Two repositories that hold one world between them are refused at the first entry
they do not share, and the refusal is all-or-nothing: there is no outcome in which the agreed world
arrives and the disagreement stays outside. The request is for the refusal to be **partial** rather than
softer.

**Not** *merge anyway*. Observation 3 gives the reason a journal cannot be admitted unagreed, and this
does not ask for it: what is asked is that the agreement be actionable without the disagreement having to
be resolved first.

**And it is the request that would move where integrity is checked** — a reading rather than a finding,
because it is composition of two documented facts and nothing here measured it. The merge never splices:
it takes the **longer journal entire**, so a sequence that admitted cleanly is replayed as the same
sequence. That is why nothing in this experiment could break a dependency between commitments, even in
principle — a dependency is resolved at admission, against the knowledge that stood
(`AxiomError::UnknownCommitment`), and a journal taken whole cannot lose the entry a later one names.

A **partial** meeting is the first operation that would have to select entries rather than take a
sequence, and selecting is where that guarantee stops holding for free. So the natural expectation —
that two repositories must not break each other's dependencies — is about this request rather than
about anything measured above.

**And [`10`](10-why-the-union-is-not-admitted.md) gives it its sharp form**, which is not about
dependencies at all. The union of two twinned repositories' knowledge *is* an admissible journal, and
handing it to a rebuild with both lineages is refused at `UnwitnessedKnowledge` — because a decision is a
fact about *exactly* the prefix that stood, and the witness is tested for set **equality** rather than
containment. So what this request needs is not a looser comparison: it is a way to say *this insertion
cannot have changed what I decided*, which nothing in the record can currently express.

---

## Request 2 — *"Take knowledge from another record without taking its intentions."*

Observation 4, from the other side. Knowledge converges by extension and intention does not converge at
all — so the two halves of a repository want different operations, and the application has one operation
that does both. A caller that wanted only the other side's knowledge would today have to accept its
lineage as well, or nothing.

**Not** two files with two APIs. The record's two halves already get different treatment inside the merge
— *knowledge appends, intention merges* — and the request is that the seam be reachable from outside,
which is the same reason exploration's seam and atomicity's seam are public.

---

## Request 3 — *"Say that two records agree, without saying it twice."*

Observation 6. Agreement between two repositories is representable only as repetition: the same world
recorded twice, because two records of one decision are two facts and the file holds records. A reader
counting the sequence gets four worlds where the archive holds three.

**Not** deduplicate the lineage. That would discard the coordinate one of the two decisions was taken at,
which the divergence experiment established is what pins a decision to the journal it belongs to. The
request is for a way to say *both of us decided this*, which is a fact neither file has a place for.

---

## Request 4 — *"Tell me where two repositories stopped being one."*

Observation 1, arriving as a need rather than as an instrument. The position of the refusal is exactly
that answer, and it is available only by attempting a merge and reading the error — so the cheapest true
thing the record can say about two repositories is something a caller obtains by asking for something
else and failing.

**Not** an ancestry API. Observation 2 established that the common history is derived and needs nothing
stored; what is missing is that the derivation is reachable only as a side effect of a refusal.

---

## Request 5 — *"Let a repository record that it met another."*

Nothing in either repository says a meeting happened. The subject gains the other's lines and cannot say
where they came from; the party is untouched and cannot tell it was read. So a merged repository is
indistinguishable from a repository whose author wrote all of it — which is the authenticity candidate's
family, arriving with a new member, and it is **not this experiment's to answer**.

Gathered in [`candidates/00-authenticity.md`](../../../candidates/00-authenticity.md), and named here so
that it is not counted as something this experiment left undone.

---

## What is not requested

**A fetch, a symmetric merge, a reference to another repository, or an authority.** All four are shapes,
all four were named in the protocol's exclusions, and none of the five requests above needs one in order
to be stated. Part B chooses among them or declines, against the criterion the coordination experiment
set and the one this protocol added — *a repair may not give one repository authority over another*.

**Anything from the engine.** Every request above is about two files and an operation over them. Nothing
here asks the ontology to know that knowledge lives in a repository, or that there is more than one —
which is the severe failure condition the protocol named and did not meet.

**And nothing asks for a merge-base.** That is the one shape a reader of the influencing system would
expect to appear here, and Observation 2 is why it does not: the record already has it, derived, and
never needed to be told.
