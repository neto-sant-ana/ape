# Result

**Confirmed** for all three lines of the hypothesis, and **Confirmed** with one clause refuted for the
prediction about what the application can express — it can express more than the protocol thought, and
still not the thing that is missing.

**Part B was not built**, and the criterion is why: every state this experiment found is refused by
name or visible in plain sight. The one that misleads a reader belongs to the authenticity candidate,
which the protocol excluded before the run.

## The answer

The question was *can two repositories meet, when nothing in either of them refers to the other?* No —
and the reason is not that they have too little in common. It is that they have the wrong thing in
common for the operation that exists.

```text
what they DO share      the history they independently said the same way, derived from
                        content, needing nothing stored and nobody told

what they DO agree      every world whose parent, cut and selection coincide — which is
about                   free, symmetric, and already askable

what refuses them       the journal, compared entire, because every standing decision is
                        witnessed against the whole prefix that stood
```

The merge-base is not missing. It is **derived**, and it was derived by the first observation of the
first experiment for a reason that had nothing to do with this. What is missing is an operation whose
subject is the agreement: the record can say what two repositories have in common, and can only act on
it by admitting a journal it has not agreed about, which it correctly refuses to do.

**And the condition is not a class of assertions.** It is worth saying which, because two readings are
natural and neither is what was measured. Agreeing about the **Events** is the condition for agreeing
about a *world* — the cut resolves against the chain, so it is exactly right there, and Observation 3
measures it. It is **not** the condition for being allowed to meet: Phase 4 refused two repositories that
agreed about **every world either of them held**, because one had admitted a **Commitment** no world
names and it put the two journals out of step. And **dependencies
between commitments** cannot break here at all, because the merge takes the longer journal entire rather
than selecting from either — a reading recorded under Request 1, where a partial meeting is the first
operation that would have to select.

What the record compares is the whole journal, positionally, entry for entry, as far as the shorter one
goes. Not Events, not dependencies, not anything a world names: everything.

**And the union is admissible, which is why the refusal is a choice.** Observation 10 measures the layer
below: the union of two twinned repositories' knowledge is one journal that admits — one of them already
holds it — and handing it to a rebuild with both lineages is refused at
`LineageError::UnwitnessedKnowledge`, naming the entry one side did not decide against. The witness is a
test of set **equality**, not of containment, so a decision taken over one prefix cannot be applied over
a longer prefix that contains it. In this case the world would have come out identical; the rule that
would allow it cannot be stated without deciding on the other side's behalf.

And the severe failure condition was **not met**, which is the most valuable thing here. If two
repositories could only meet by one being declared authoritative, this record would be centralized. They
cannot be, because there is no asymmetry for an authority to occupy: both sides derive the common history
independently, from what they said. Whatever a meeting needs, it does not need one of them to be the real
one.

## Against each criterion

1. **Every measurement is a call over two directories in one process.** No network, no clone, no thread.
   A meeting is a call, and which repository is its subject is a value the phase chooses.
2. **Both repositories are founded from one statement of the subject**, so a shared base is shared by
   content — and Phase 2 shows it, by identity, with nothing having run between them.
3. **Compared by value against each repository alone and against their union.** Every outcome is a
   `State`: entries, decisions, worlds, and the level each world intends — 300, 260 and 230, three
   numbers written before the run, so an outcome is named rather than counted.
4. **Where a meeting is refused, the coordinate is named and related to what the two shared.** Position
   0 for disjoint, 13 for a shared base, and Phase 2 derives the same number from the journals without
   running anything.
5. **The three relations are exercised in both directions, and the asymmetry is a measurement.** This
   criterion caught a real gap: the first version of the extending phase ran one direction only, and a
   mutation of the merge's *other* branch stayed green. Both directions are now measured, and the
   asymmetry is reported as *the same value, a different holder*.
6. **What a world's identity pins is stated as a rule rather than an example.** Everything except the
   admissions its selection names and the Events its cut resolves against, with both visible edges
   measured.
7. **Nothing was added to the APE engine.** `core/` is untouched across the branch: zero files.
8. **Part B was not built, against the criterion, and the absence is the result.** Observation 9 weighs
   every state found and says which one would have qualified and why it belongs elsewhere.
9. **The eight earlier experiments' conclusions stand.** Coordination's *intention merges* — the one at
   risk — holds and is sharpened: it merges records of decisions, and two records of one world are two
   facts. Details below.

**Criteria 4 and 6 are the experiment**, and both hold.

**Failure conditions.** Neither severe one was met: nothing outside the record was introduced, no
repository was given authority over another, and nothing in the engine learned that knowledge lives in a
repository — let alone that there is more than one. Of the ordinary ones: no measurement needs a network,
a clone or a thread; every outcome is reported from what the record holds; no repair was chosen at all;
`Diverged` is reported as refusing the shape it was built to refuse, and its coordinate is reported as a
measurement rather than as a complaint; the asymmetry is C5's answer rather than a remark; and two worlds
are called the same only where their identities compare equal.

**And one honest limit.** The two repositories in this arrangement are the same author's, on the same
disk, founded by one function called twice. That is what makes the measurement possible and it is also
the whole of what *two repositories* means here. Nothing about trust, provenance or distance was
measured, and the moment either is, the authenticity candidate is the experiment being run.

## Findings that outlast the verdict

**The common ancestor is derived, and nothing copied it.** Two repositories share exactly the history
they independently said the same way. The influencing system needs a merge-base object; this needs
nothing, because an identity is what admitting produced. Nine experiments have been building on a
distributed property while measuring one repository at a time.

**Two repositories decided one world, and nothing coordinated it.** A `Thesis` is identified by its
parent, its cut and its selection, all content-addressed — so agreement is what content addressing does
when two records say the same thing.

**And it is conditional, at the cut.** A `KnowledgeCut` is `(known_at, event_head)` and the head resolves
against the Event chain that stood. The same decision over knowledge differing by one Event produces a
different world. So nothing agrees by accident, and the free agreement depends on exactly what the merge
refuses.

**The arrow runs one way.** Agreement about a world implies agreement about the knowledge under it.
Agreement about knowledge implies nothing about the worlds over it: a repository holding another's whole
journal agreed about one of its two worlds, because carrying knowledge is not intending anything by it.
*A shared journal is not a shared lineage.*

**A world's identity is blind to the journal.** Everything except its selection and its cut is invisible
to it — which is not a gap but what makes the identity mean the world rather than the file. And it is
where the meeting fails, measured on two **twinned** repositories: same base, same plan, the same lineage
by identity, each holding every world the other holds — and refused in both directions, because one of
them admitted an inflow no world names and that put the two journals **out of step**. A positional
comparison cannot be blind to that, and a world identity cannot see it.

**A refusal's coordinate is a measurement.** The position where two journals diverge is the size of what
they share, and it is the cheapest true thing the record can say about two repositories — obtainable only
by asking for something else and failing.

**A repository holds records of decisions, and a decision is not its world.** Two records of one world
are two facts, *decided here* and *decided there*, so agreement between two repositories is representable
only as repetition: four decisions, three worlds, one of them answered for twice, and the duplicate
visible in plain view.

**The record answers what it cannot act on.** `converge::holds`, built so a party could ask whether what
it decided survived, is a symmetric read-only query for what two repositories agree about. The operation
that would put the agreement together refuses. What is absent is not the knowledge and not a way to find
it — it is an operation whose subject is the agreement.

**A missing capability and a misleading state are not the same defect.** Nine experiments have applied
one criterion for a second half, and this is the first time it came up empty — not because nothing is
missing, but because everything missing here is about what a writer can do. A criterion built to protect
readers will decline to fix that, correctly.

## What changed in the experiments before it

**Nothing was overturned.**

**Reconstruction's first observation acquired a second purpose.** *Knowledge is reconstructed by
replaying admissions, and an entry is addressed by what admitting produced.* The reason given was that an
address survives reordering, splitting and re-encoding where an offset survives none of them. It is also
the whole of what two repositories share, which nobody had reason to notice for nine experiments.

**Coordination's `holds` is doing a second job**, and its docstring now says so: pointed at another
repository's world, it is the only thing in the application a second repository is answerable to.

**Coordination's *intention merges* is sharpened rather than moved.** It merges records, and two records
of one world are both kept — correctly, since neither is redundant as a record. What follows is that the
merged repository answers for one world twice, which nothing had produced before because nothing had two
records of one world.

**Contention supplied this experiment's central prediction.** *The merge accepts extension and refuses
divergence* is what makes C1 a consequence rather than a suspicion, and the coordinate it names is what
turns the refusal into an instrument.

**Divergence's rule is why one repair is not available.** A decision filed under a coordinate it did not
have reads back as a different lineage — which is why deduplicating the merged lineage by world is not
the fix for the duplicate.

## Variables the protocol left open

**Whether a second repository is somebody else's.** Untouched, deliberately, and now with a member added
to the authenticity candidate: a merged repository is indistinguishable from one whose author wrote every
line.

**What a repository owes another it has met.** Nothing records that a meeting happened, so the question
is still downstream of an operation that does not exist.

**More than two.** Untouched.

**Cost.** Deferred by ten experiments now, with one term added: a meeting rebuilds both lineages in
memory before writing either.

## What the result does not cover

**A network, a protocol, a transport.** Excluded, and nothing here is about latency, partition or
ordering between machines.

**A clone.** Excluded on purpose — adding one would have answered C2 by construction.

**Whether a partial meeting is possible.** Request 1, and the nearest unanswered question after this one:
the refusal is all-or-nothing, and nothing here measured whether it has to be.

**Authenticity.** Named for the eighth time, and this time with a new face.

**Benchmarking.** No timing was taken.

# Architectural Consequences

## Established for the CLI

* **Two repositories share the history they independently said the same way.** Derived from content,
  needing nothing stored and neither of them told.
* **Two repositories agree about a world exactly where they agreed about the knowledge under it**, and
  `converge::holds` is how a caller finds out — symmetric, read-only, and already there.
* **A meeting is refused at the first entry two journals do not share**, and that position is the size of
  what they have in common.
* **A world's identity is blind to everything but its selection and its cut**, so agreement about a world
  is no guarantee of anything about the files.
* **Acting on a meeting is asymmetric**: the subject gains both lines, the party is untouched and cannot
  tell.
* **A merged repository answers for a world both sides decided twice**, and the duplicate is visible.

## Candidates for later experiments

* **The witness** — Requests 1, 2 and 3, which are **one** candidate and not three. Each is blocked by
  the same line: a decision is recorded against what *stood*, the check is set equality rather than
  containment, and nothing in the record says what a decision is actually *about*. Record a decision
  against what it depends on and a partial meeting becomes statable, knowledge becomes takeable without
  its lineage, and the duplicate of Observation 6 never arises. Gathered in
  [`candidates/05-witness.md`](../../../candidates/05-witness.md), where it is the **fourth** arrival:
  the exploration experiment asked the same question as *cost* — a decision should cost what the decision
  is, not what has been admitted by the time it is taken.
* **Authenticity**, with a new face: a merged repository does not say it met anything.
* **Whether a repository may say how many writers it admits** — unchanged since contention, and now with
  a second reason, because a second repository is a second writer that never asked.
* **Cost as an absolute**, deferred by ten experiments.
