# Observation 5 — A world's identity is blind to the journal

C4 predicted that a world's identity does not pin the journal it came from. Phase 4 measured it, and the
result is a rule rather than a case.

One repository admitted a commitment no world selects — an inflow of 25 that nothing proposes and no cut
can name. Its journal grew by one entry. **Every world it decided is the world it decided without that
entry**, by identity, and its lineage is the same length it was.

```text
journal      14 entries  →  15 entries
lineage      2 worlds    →  the same 2 worlds, same identities
```

A `Thesis` names a parent, a cut and a selection. A journal is none of those. So the closed set of
differences a world's identity is blind to is not a list of cases — it is everything except two things,
and both were measured at their edge:

```text
an admission the selection names        VISIBLE      two sides proposing different plans
                                                     decide different worlds (Obs 3)

an Event                                VISIBLE      the cut resolves against the chain, so the
                                                     same instant addresses a different head (Obs 3)

anything else                           BLIND        a commitment nobody selects, and by the same
                                                     reasoning a role, an agent, an action or a
                                                     statement nothing selected refers to
```

> **A world's identity is derived from what it reasons about. Everything else in the record is invisible
> to it, and that is not a gap — it is what makes the identity mean the world rather than the file.**

## And this is where the meeting fails

The consequence is the experiment's centre, and it needed one more relation than the protocol listed —
because the obvious arrangement cannot show it.

Two repositories that share a base and each admit a plan of their own agree about the base world and are
refused. But they would be refused with or without any unnamed knowledge: each admitted something of its
own, so the journals diverge at the entry after the base for the ordinary reason. That arrangement
demonstrates nothing about the blindness above, and the first version of this observation claimed it did.

So the sides are **twinned**: one base, and the *same plan* on both. Their lineages are then the same
lineage, by identity, and there is nothing of their own left for a refusal to be about. Then one side
admits the inflow of 25 — knowledge no world on either side names.

```text
twinned.left.worlds  ==  twinned.right.worlds        they agree about EVERY world either holds
agreed(right, left.worlds)  ==  2                    and each holds all of the other's, by identity
met(left, right)     ==  Diverged at 13              and they are refused
met(right, left)     ==  Diverged at 13              in both directions
```

What is at position 13 is the whole mechanism, and the refusal is not about a *kind* of entry:

```text
left  [13] = the inflow of 25       named by no world on either side
right [13] = the plan of 40         named by a world BOTH sides hold
left  [14] = the plan of 40         the same entry, one place later
```

The two journals say different things in the same place, and the extra knowledge is what put them out
of step. A positional comparison has no way to be blind to that — and the world identities have no way
to see it. Both facts are correct at once: the merge compares journals because standing decisions are
witnessed against them; the identity ignores journals because a world is what it reasons about. Neither
is a defect, and together they mean *two repositories that agree about everything cannot act on the
agreement*.

## What it does not say

That the merge should ignore unnamed knowledge. It cannot: the witness a decision carries is over the
whole prefix that stood, not over the entries the world selects, and the convergence experiment declined
to call that witness redundant for reasons three experiments have since used. A merge that skipped
unnamed entries would be admitting a journal against which standing witnesses are false.

What is left is not a weaker comparison. It is an operation that takes the agreement as its subject, and
[`07`](07-the-record-answers-what-it-cannot-act-on.md) measures how close the application already is to
having one.
