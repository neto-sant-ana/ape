# Observation 4 — Extension does not imply agreement, and a phase predicted otherwise

This one is a prediction of the run corrected in the open, which the protocol requires and which is
worth more here than the prediction would have been.

Phase 5 was written expecting that under `Extending` — where the right repository's journal **is** the
left's, extended — the right would hold both of the left's worlds. It holds one.

```text
relation      worlds the other repository holds, of this one's two
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Disjoint      0
Shared        1        the base
Extending     1        the base, and not the left's own world
```

The reason is that `Extending` is a relation between **journals**. The right side holds every entry the
left holds, including the commitment the left's own world proposes — and it never decided anything about
it. Carrying knowledge is not intending anything by it, which is the ordinary state of a journal and was
the whole point of the unwitnessed tail in the atomicity subject.

So the arrow Observation 3 established runs one way only:

```text
agreement about a world      →      agreement about the knowledge under it       HOLDS
agreement about knowledge    →      agreement about the worlds over it           DOES NOT
```

## Why the mistake was worth making

It was the same conflation the word *meeting* invites. Two repositories with one journal between them
look like they have met, and what they have is a shared substrate and two separate deliberations over
it. Knowledge is a sequence and converges by extension; intention is a tree and does not converge at
all — it branches, and the branches are the point.

Stated so that it is about the two files rather than about this arrangement:

> **A shared journal is not a shared lineage.** The first is a prefix relation and can be complete; the
> second is a set of independent decisions, and two records can hold the same knowledge and disagree
> about every world above it without either being wrong.

## What it does not say

That agreement about worlds is rare. Observation 3 measured the opposite: it is free, wherever the
knowledge and the intent coincide. What this adds is that it cannot be *inferred* — a caller holding two
repositories that share a journal has learned nothing about what they agree, and has to ask.

Observation 7 measures what asking costs, which is nothing.
