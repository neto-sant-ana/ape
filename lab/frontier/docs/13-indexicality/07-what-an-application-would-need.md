# Observation 7 — What an application would need, and none of it built

Requests, in the vocabulary of the need rather than of a design. A friction is evidence of a want,
not of a need, and four of these are wants that four results have now asked for in five ways.

**Request 1 — a refusal that says which side is missing.** `UnknownEntry` names an address and is
returned both when a coordinate is wrong in the right journal and when a coordinate is right in the
wrong journal. Those are different faults with different repairs, and a reader is handed the half of
the pair that was never in doubt. The record has the other half in hand at the moment it refuses: it
knows whether it holds any entry the witness names.

**Request 2 — an outcome for *I cannot answer for this decision*.** Distinct from refusing, and the
protocol left it open as a question. The measurement says the two are currently the same value: a
record holding nothing refuses a true decision exactly as it refuses a false one. A record that could
say *this is not about me* would be saying something it can derive — the witness and its own addresses
are disjoint — and it would stop a reader looking for corruption that is not there.

**Request 3 — a comparison over journals that is about membership rather than sequence.** `converge`
refuses a reordering the record's own reader accepts. Sound and not complete, and the incomplete side
costs a party an attempt rather than a decision, so this is the smallest of the five.

**Request 4 — somewhere for an instant to be answerable to.** The largest and the one nothing in the
record can supply. A recording instant is derived from nothing, so no guard weighs it; the only thing
that ever notices is a world that was derived from it earlier, and any writer rewriting that world
erases the notice. What would answer it is outside the record, which is the authenticity candidate,
and this experiment reached its boundary rather than crossing it.

**Request 5 — a way to hand a decision over at all.** There is none. `converge` merges two whole
repositories and `holds` answers about an identity; nothing takes one decision. Every question the row
still has about two records exchanging deliberation is asking for this, and the measurement here is
what it would cost: the receiver must already hold the content, and the instants it would have to be
told are unverifiable by anyone.

## What was measured and is not a request

**The pin is completable, and the record already writes two thirds of it.** The coordinate and the
witness are there; what is missing is an instant per witnessed entry, and the journal holds every one
of them already. Nobody needs a new value invented — this is a question about where an existing value
is written down, and that is why Part B was a real possibility rather than a speculation.

**And it is not proposed.** Observation 6's reason: the state a completed pin would exclude is not
reachable through any operation, and the operation that would reach it refuses first.

## What this experiment did not measure

**A receiver holding part.** The three receivers hold everything, everything-but-one-instant, and
nothing. The obvious reading — that what a receiver can check is exactly what it holds — is consistent
with three points and established by none of them. The shape that would test it is two records sharing
a base and diverging after it, which experiment 12 arranged for another purpose.

**More than one decision travelling.** One decision, one coordinate. A lineage arriving as a sequence
raises an ordering question this says nothing about.

**Whether a decision should be portable.** The row has assumed it wants this. The price is now
measured; whether it is worth paying is not a question a phase answers.
