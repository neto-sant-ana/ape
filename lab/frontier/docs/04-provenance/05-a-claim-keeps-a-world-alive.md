# Observation 5 — A claim keeps a world alive that nothing else refers to

Phase 6 asked what the claim couples, and the answer is not a performance cost.

The adopted world does **not** descend from the plan it took its intention from. They sit on
separate branches — that is what made the transfer a transfer. So the claim names a world the
claiming world has no ancestry to, and re-asking the question on every read means that world has to
still be there.

Measured by pruning it. A lineage with the broad plan's decision simply not written down:

```text
without the claim    the adopted world rebuilds, identical, and the repository reads
with the claim       the repository refuses: the world the claim asks about is gone
```

Every world the adopted one needs is still decided. Its ancestry is untouched, its identity is the
same identity. What breaks is only the ability to check a claim about a branch nobody kept.

> A line of thinking cannot be discarded once anything claims to have adopted from it.

Not because the worlds need it, which is measured above, but because a claim re-asked on every read
keeps alive a world that nothing else refers to.

## Which lands on a question four protocols have deferred

Abandoned siblings have been left unmodelled by every protocol in this sequence, each time on
purpose and each time noted. This is the first measurement of what provenance would cost that
question, and it costs it everything: the only worlds a repository could ever prune are the ones
nothing points at, and provenance points at exactly the ones a planner would want to drop — the
alternatives that were considered and not taken.

That is a cost with no offsetting benefit in this boundary, since Part A established there is
nothing to buy.

## Phase 7, for completeness

The claimed arrangement crosses a process whole: five readings, identical, plus the literals written
down beforehand. The claim changed no world, which is the same fact Phase 2 measured and Phase 4
measured again, arriving a third time from the far side of a process boundary.
