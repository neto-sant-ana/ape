# Provenance

## Abstract

The convergence experiment moved an intention from one line of thinking into another and proved
the result reconstructible. It also measured what the result forgot.

A transfer applied is an ordinary fork. It records what was introduced and not that another line
decided it first, so the Base and the Source do not survive into the world they produced. Asked
how many transfers would produce that exact world, the answer came back **three**.

The engine has one notion of why an ancestry edge exists, and it is deliberate: a commitment
absent from a parent was either introduced by a decision or imposed by history, and fork and
advance are kept disjoint so that nothing has to guess which. What no layer models is a third
thing — *decided, by adopting what another line had decided.*

> *Why did this intention arrive here?*

---

## Question

The question splits in two, and they are asked in order because the second is only interesting if
the first survives.

**Is it necessary?** A record that cannot answer a question nobody asks is complete. So: what is
actually asked of this repository that provenance would answer, and of those, what does the
record already answer, and what can a reader recover by searching?

**Is it possible?** Not *can a field be added* — anything can be added. A provenance claim that
nothing checks is a story, and this laboratory has spent three experiments establishing that a
derived value written and never compared is worse than one absent. So: can a record say where an
intention came from **in a form reconstruction can refuse**?

The two are separable, and the honest outcomes include *not necessary*, *not checkable*, and both.

---

## Hypothesis

Two claims, one per question, each separately refutable.

```text
necessity    there is a question about a world that the record cannot answer
             and no search over the repository recovers

possibility  where an intention came from is recordable as a claim that
             reconstruction refuses when it is false
```

The first is the one this experiment expects to have the hardest time with. Convergence found
three explanations for one world and observed that two of them have the applied world itself as
their Source — the answer read backwards rather than a rival account. If a rule excludes those,
the count is one, provenance is recoverable by search, and the necessity claim is refuted.

That would be a complete result and a good one. It is written here so that finding it cannot be
presented afterwards as something else.

---

## Motivation

Three experiments have been about whether a record can be **reproduced**. This one is about
whether it can be **read**.

The distinction is not decorative. A repository that reproduces every world and explains none of
them is exactly as correct and considerably less useful: a reader who wants to know why a
commitment is selected in some world can derive that it is, and cannot derive why anyone put it
there. Convergence stated that boundary plainly and declined to cross it, because reproduction
was what it was testing.

There is a second reason, and it is the one with consequences. The convergence protocol left a
question open that nobody could answer from the record:

> *If a Source lineage is later found to have been corrupted, what happens to what was
> transferred out of it?*

An application that cannot say which worlds inherited an intention from a discredited line cannot
answer that at all. Whether anyone will need to is exactly what the first half of this experiment
is for.

---

## Experimental Boundary

This experiment exercises what a record says about where an intention came from.

It includes:

* an arrangement in which more than one transfer would produce the same world, if one can be
  built;
* whatever the current record already answers, established by asking rather than by assuming;
* a provenance claim of some shape, and reconstruction's ability to refuse a false one;
* the corroboration discipline as it now stands, applied to whatever this experiment adds;
* the cost of whatever is added, named.

It deliberately excludes:

* authenticity — who wrote a record, as opposed to what it claims. Unchanged and still open;
* concurrent writers, and two repositories meeting;
* snapshots, indexes, and any measurement of what replay costs;
* fractional magnitudes;
* mutable named references;
* production-oriented user experience.

Those concerns may become later experiments.

They must not influence the structure introduced here unless this experiment itself requires
them.

---

## Experimental Subject

**A subject of this experiment's own**, for the reason the previous three give: a concluded
experiment keeps its subject unchanged, and a published result whose subject moved underneath it
is a result nobody can run again.

What this one must express is one thing the convergence subject could not:

```text
two transfers, neither degenerate, producing one world
```

Degenerate means the Source is the world being explained, or descends from it. Convergence found
two of those and they are not rival accounts — they are the answer read backwards. A subject that
offers two **independent** Sources whose transfers resolve to the same thing against the same
Target is what makes the necessity question decidable by measurement instead of by argument.

Whether such an arrangement exists at all is not assumed. If Phase 1 cannot build one, that is a
finding and it very nearly settles the experiment: an applied world that only ever has one
non-degenerate explanation is a world whose provenance is recoverable by search.

Everything else stays as thin as the previous subjects: integers, one quantifiable resource, no
dependencies unless the procedure demands them.

```text
repository = empty

```

## Procedure

### Part A — Is it necessary?

#### Phase 1 — Ambiguity

Build an arrangement in which two non-degenerate transfers produce one world, and count the
explanations for every world in it.

If it cannot be built, record why, and record what that implies: search recovers provenance, and
the necessity claim is in serious trouble before Part A is half done.

---

#### Phase 2 — Exhaust what the record already answers

List what a reader might ask about a world, and for each, establish by asking rather than by
argument whether the current repository answers it.

```text
what does this world select                    derived
what did the decision that made it ask for     recorded
which world did it extend                      recorded
when was it taken                              recorded
which line of thinking did the intention       ?
  come from
```

The convergence experiment's proposed rule is tested here rather than assumed: does excluding
Sources that descend from the world being explained leave exactly one candidate? Phase 1's
arrangement is what makes that question answerable rather than rhetorical.

---

#### Phase 3 — The consequence of not knowing

Discredit a Source lineage — by whatever means the repository already admits — and ask what a
reader can say about the worlds that took intention out of it.

This is where necessity becomes concrete or dissolves. If the reader can identify them anyway,
Part A ends refuted, and the experiment continues into Part B knowing that whatever it builds is
not required.

---

### Part B — Is it possible?

Part B runs whatever Part A concludes. A cost measured for something that turned out unnecessary
is knowledge; an unmeasured cost is the reason a question comes back.

What Part B builds does **not** stay in the repository unless Part A said it must.

---

#### Phase 4 — Record the claim

Decide what shape a provenance claim takes, and answer both of the corroboration experiment's
questions for every datum it adds:

> *What becomes impossible if this is not preserved?*
> *What compares it, on every read?*

Two shapes are live and the procedure chooses between them rather than inheriting a choice:

```text
stated    a fork as it stands, plus the question it answered
computed  a decision that records only the question, and derives what it introduced
```

The second is the more interesting and the more dangerous. It would make a world's content the
output of Synthesis rather than a statement in the record — which is the thing three experiments
have been careful not to do.

---

#### Phase 5 — Refuse a false claim

A claim nothing checks is a story. Write a repository whose provenance claim is well-formed and
false, and require reconstruction to refuse it and name what disagreed.

If no false claim can be constructed that reconstruction detects, the possibility claim is
refuted and the shape chosen in Phase 4 is a story with better formatting.

---

#### Phase 6 — What the claim couples

Name what now depends on what. A recorded question that is re-asked on every read ties the
repository to Synthesis continuing to answer it the same way; a recorded answer ties it to
nothing and says less.

Measure the coupling rather than describing it: change what the answer would be, and see what the
repository does.

---

#### Phase 7 — Terminate, rebuild, compare

The whole arrangement, through a fresh process, against what a living one recorded — and against
literals written down before the run.

---

## Success Criteria

The two halves are judged separately, and the experiment is confirmed only if both hold.

**Necessity**

1. The number of non-degenerate explanations for at least one world is greater than one, measured
   rather than argued.
2. There is a question, stated concretely and asked of the repository, that the record does not
   answer and no search recovers.
3. The consequence of not answering it is demonstrated on a repository rather than described.

**Possibility**

4. What a decision records is stated as a closed set, and anything added answers both of Phase
   4's questions.
5. A well-formed false provenance claim is refused, and the refusal names what disagreed.
6. Every persisted derived value is compared on every read, walked as a closed list rather than
   argued.
7. The arrangement reproduces whole through a fresh process.
8. What the claim couples is measured and stated.
9. No provenance-specific concern is introduced into the APE engine.
10. The four earlier experiments' conclusions stand, or the change is recorded as a result of
    this one.

---

## Failure Conditions

The hypothesis is refuted, or narrowed, if:

* every world has exactly one non-degenerate explanation, so search recovers what a record would
  have said;
* every question provenance would answer is already answered, or answerable, without it;
* no false provenance claim can be made that reconstruction detects;
* recording provenance requires the engine to understand where an intention came from;
* the claim can only be checked by persisting something nothing else compares.

**A refuted necessity is the most likely outcome and is a complete result.** It says a repository
of decisions is enough, which is a stronger statement about this architecture than another
confirmation would be. The implementation must not rescue necessity by inventing a consumer
nobody has.

---

## Variables Deliberately Left Open

### Whether provenance belongs to the decision or beside it

A field on a fork, a decision of its own, or a separate record that refers to one. The three
differ in what a reader must read to see it and in what a corruption must move to hide it.

### Whether a claim names the report or the question

Base and Source are the question. The difference, the status and the conflicts are the answer.
The corroboration rule points at recording the question and re-deriving the answer, and this
procedure tests that rather than assuming it.

### Partial provenance

If only transfers explain themselves, ordinary forks still do not. Whether a record where some
decisions carry a reason and others do not is coherent — or worse than none — is not settled
here.

### Abandoned siblings

Still not modelled. Four protocols have now left it open, which is starting to be a statement of
its own.

---

## Methodological Constraint

This experiment follows one implementation rule:

> *Do not introduce an abstraction intended to solve an experiment that has not yet been
> performed.*

Structure may be introduced only when required by the current experimental procedure.

The previous experiments' conclusions are not revised here. Where this one finds something that
would have changed them, it is recorded as a finding of this experiment, against the
implementation as it then stood.

And one editorial rule, because the previous experiment needed it four times: before recording
anything as a finding, ask whether it is written in a docstring in `core/`, and ask whether the
behaviour being described would be better the other way. A defined behaviour reported as friction
sends the next reader looking for a repair nobody needs.

## Expected Pressure Points

### Necessity is the hard half

Every instinct says a record should explain itself. Instinct is not a consumer. The procedure
asks for a question somebody actually asks, and the arrangement is built so that the answer can
be *no*.

### Computed content

If a decision records the question and derives what it introduced, the world's identity becomes
the output of an algorithm rather than a statement in the record. Three experiments have kept
content stated for exactly this reason, and a fourth reversing it needs more than convenience.

### A claim about the past that the past cannot refuse

A world record disagrees because the world can be rebuilt. A provenance claim is about *why*, and
why is not rebuildable — only the report is. So the check has a specific shape: the claim is
refusable exactly insofar as it predicts something derivable, and any part of it that predicts
nothing is unfalsifiable by construction.

### The degenerate exclusion

Convergence proposed excluding Sources that descend from the world being explained and did not
test it. If the rule is sound, search wins. If it is unsound, the counterexample is this
experiment's first real finding.

### Provenance and authenticity are not the same thing

A record can say where an intention came from and be lying, and no amount of internal agreement
closes that. The corroboration experiment measured it, this one inherits it, and neither is
entitled to describe a provenance claim as trustworthy.

## Observations

Each observation is recorded as its own numbered document beside this one, as the experiment
produces it.

Record facts rather than decisions retroactively presented as inevitable.

Useful observations include:

* questions that turned out to be answerable already;
* consumers that dissolved on inspection;
* claims that could be written and not refused;
* places where explaining a record and reproducing it pulled in different directions;
* assumptions the previous experiments made about their own subjects.

Where possible, record the smallest reproducing case.

## Open Questions

* What does a discredited lineage owe to the ones that took intention from it?
* Does a transfer have an identity, and would provenance be it?
* If provenance is a claim, who is entitled to make it?
* What does a lineage that was discarded owe to one that was kept?

These are candidates for later experiments.

They are not requirements for this one.

---

## Experimental Principle

Reconstruction asked whether meaning survives.

Divergence asked whether deliberation survives.

Corroboration asked whether the record can be trusted without being believed.

Convergence asked whether two lines of deliberation can still reach each other.

```text
This one asks whether a record that can be
rebuilt perfectly is thereby understood — and
is willing to answer that it does not need to be.
```

The experiment will determine what a repository must record for that to remain true.
