# Observation 4 — A claim is checkable exactly where it predicts, and it predicts the wrong thing

Part A ended refuted. Part B ran anyway, because the protocol said a cost measured for something
unnecessary is knowledge and an unmeasured one is why a question comes back.

## One shape was eliminated on principle

The protocol left two live. A decision that records only the question and **derives** what it
introduced is rejected without being built: if the record holds no content, whatever Synthesis
answers *is* the content, and there is no second representation for a reader to disagree with.
Unfalsifiable by construction is the one thing the corroboration rule forbids outright.

So the fork stays stated and the claim is an annotation beside it — which is also the only place
Phase 2 left available:

```json
{"decides":"fork","extends":"291e3f84…","omitted":[],"introduced":["b3387f9a…"],
 "after":"…","witness":[…],
 "from":{"base":"175e358d…","source":"329ff7bc…"}}
```

Optional, and absent from every decision that was not chosen from a transfer. A planner with their
own reasons has nothing to say there, and saying nothing is not the same as claiming to have come
from nowhere. None of the four earlier suites noticed the field, because it is not written when it
is not there.

## Both of the two questions, answered

*What becomes impossible if this is not preserved?* **Nothing.** Part A measured that three times
over, and Phase 4 measured it once more directly: the claimed repository and the unclaimed one
reconstruct the same world.

*What compares it, on every read?* The claim names a Base and a Source; the decision names the
Target and the change. So the question is asked again inside reconstruction, and a claim whose
transfer is not this change is refused by name.

## And then the whole space of claims was written down

Every Base and Source the record could name, one repository each, classified by what the read makes
of it. Five outcomes, and only one of them matters:

```text
incoherent    the Base is not an ancestor of both        refused
unwritable    the Source is the world being decided      refused
impossible    the transfer from that Source is empty     refused
contradicted  the transfer asks for something else       never occurred
accepted      (ancestor, narrow) and (ancestor, broad)   two, one of them false
```

**`unwritable` is a finding.** A claim cannot name the world its own decision is about, because at
the moment the check runs that world has not been decided and is not in the archive to be named. The
degenerate-exclusion rule convergence proposed and never tested turns out to need no rule at all —
it falls out of when things happen.

**`contradicted` never occurred, and that is the refutation.** In this arrangement there is no
Base-and-Source pair that is possible and predicts a different change. Every claim that could have
happened predicts the same transfer, because the transfer is the only thing that arrived.

So the accepted set is exactly Phase 1's two rival accounts, reached from the other direction: the
check accepts precisely what a search would have found, and separates them no better.

## What that says, stated carefully

Nothing was built wrong. The check does everything a check could do: it rejects the incoherent, the
impossible, and the temporally absurd, and it names each. What it cannot enter is the space of
claims that are all consistent with the record — and that space is exactly what provenance was for.

> A claim is refusable exactly insofar as it predicts something derivable. This one predicts the
> transfer, and the transfer is what every rival account has in common.

The possibility half is refuted, and not by a shortcoming. It is refuted by the same fact that
refuted necessity, arriving a third time: **what a transfer moves is all a transfer leaves.**

## What was not measured

Whether a claim could predict something *other* than the transfer, and so become falsifiable. It
would have to predict something derivable that differs between rival Sources — and Phase 1 showed
the rivals differ only in their own selections, which the receiving record has no business holding.
That is the shape of an authenticity claim rather than a corroboration one: a signature over who
wrote the record, which this boundary excludes and the corroboration experiment already named.
