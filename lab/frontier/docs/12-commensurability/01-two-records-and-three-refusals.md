# Observation 1 — Two records founded apart, and the three refusals reproduced by value

The arrangement is the collision experiment's twinning with one thing added, and the addition is the
whole experiment: two insertions that look alike and are not.

```text
shared, admitted independently by both records, identical entry for entry
  F   receive 400   committed day 2, recorded day 2
  E₀  Event settling F, occurred day 3, recorded day 3          14 entries

one adds     H   spend 50, recorded day 4   and  Eₕ  Event settling H, recorded day 5
other adds   P   spend 70, recorded day 6
```

```text
                              entries   worlds       settled / intended
  one    { D₀, D₁ }              16        2         400/400  then  350/350
  other  { D₀, D₂ }              15        2         400/400  then  400/330
  union                          17        —
```

`D₀` is the same decision on both sides but for the party claiming it, so the two records decided one
world by identity with nothing between them. Measured rather than arranged: the two bases are equal
address for address, and each record rebuilt on its own produces the shared world first.

**And `D₂` is a genesis rather than a fork, which is load-bearing.** A fork inherits its parent's cut,
so an Event admitted in front of it cannot move anything. An arrangement built out of forks could not
have failed, and the mutation below is what says so rather than the sentence.

## The three refusals

Reproduced **by name** before anything changes, so that a change in outcome later is attributable.

**Request 1 — the only operation there is refuses all-or-nothing.** `converge` refuses at position
14, which is the size of what the two records have in common, and the first record is left exactly as
it was. That is 09's Observation 1 arriving as the block rather than as the instrument.

**Request 1's sharp form — the union with both lineages is refused by the witness.** The journal
admits entire, every coordinate resolves, and one of the two records is refused with
`UnwitnessedKnowledge` naming an entry that belongs to the other. Which record is refused is not a
coin toss and Observation 4 is about that.

**Request 3 — agreement is representable only as repetition.** The union's lineage holds the agreed
decision twice: same decision, same coordinate, different party. Nothing deduplicates it, and nothing
should — the coordinate each was taken at is what pins it to a journal.

## What the arrangement can and cannot fail

The literals are asserted against each other before anything runs, and three of them are the
falsifiability condition: the changed world has to answer differently from **every** world either
record decided, or a phase reporting *a decision was changed* would be reporting a coincidence.
`(350, 280)` is that world, and it matches none of `(400, 400)`, `(350, 350)`, `(400, 330)`.

**Seen red by mutating the subject rather than the suite.** Turning `D₂` from a genesis into a fork
over the agreed world makes six phases fail, and the decisive one reports
`[("one", [0, 1]), ("other", [0, 1])]` — a partial meeting carrying **everything**. That is the
arrangement that could not have failed, and the mutation is what found it.
