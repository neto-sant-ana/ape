# Observation 3 — A partial meeting is statable, and recording order decides who loses a world

**M3 holds.** The union is one journal, and each decision either produces the world its record
recorded against it or does not. Three of the four do.

```text
one     D₀  the shared world      carried        D₁  its own      carried
other   D₀  the shared world      carried        D₂  its own      LEFT OVER
```

Which is the answer to *put together the part we agree about, and tell me what is left*, and the
thing left over is one decision rather than a repository. The agreement is actionable without the
disagreement having been resolved, which is exactly what experiment 09 asked for and could not state.

## Who pays is decided by neither record

The asymmetry is not a tie-break and it is not a preference:

> A journal is a sequence and recording is monotonic across admission, so the entries recorded
> **earliest** are the ones that land in front of the other record's coordinate. The record whose
> knowledge is dated later is the one whose own world a meeting cannot carry.

Measured: the first record's entries sort first in the union, it keeps both of its worlds, and the
second keeps one.

**And the deciding field is the one experiment 11 measured a merge adopting.** `recorded_at` belongs
to no identity, so two records cannot compare it by address; it is what orders the union; and here it
is what decides which of two parties loses a decision. Three experiments have now found that field
deciding something nobody chose it to decide.

## The witness is sound and it is incomplete

**M4's first half, and the boundary experiment 10 measured, re-measured here against a comparison
that sits beside the witness rather than in its place.**

```text
insertion                       the witness    re-derivation

the Commitment alone            refuses        unchanged, both worlds carried
the Commitment and the Event    refuses        changed
```

So the witness **never admits a changed world** — which is what makes it safe and is the strongest
thing anybody has said about it — and it refuses unchanged ones, which is what makes 09's request
legitimate rather than a wish for a looser check. Set equality is a sufficient condition for a world
being unchanged and is not a necessary one, and the gap between the two is exactly the capability
three results have been asking for.
