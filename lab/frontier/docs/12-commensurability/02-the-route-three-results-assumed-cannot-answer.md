# Observation 2 — The claim is derivable, and not from what a decision is *about*

Two insertions, both belonging to the other record, both in front of this record's coordinate, both
refused by the witness today. Exactly one of them matters.

```text
                      what it is                        can it have changed D₂?

H    spend 50         a Commitment nothing selects      NO — D₂ produces the same world
                                                        by identity against the extended journal

Eₕ   settles H        an Event nothing selects either   YES — D₂'s cut resolves its head from an
                                                        instant, so the head moves E₀ → Eₕ
```

So **M1 and M2 hold together**: the claim is derivable, and it decides in both directions. What it is
derived from is a coordinate, a journal and a decision, with neither writer consulted and nothing
added to either record.

## What the Event does, and it is worse than moving an identity

`Eₕ` settles `H`, and `H` is a commitment `other` never selected. The frozen past is
`Settled(head) ∪ Ancestors(…)`, so when the head moves, `H` enters the frozen half of a world that
does not hold it in its selection — and the settled level moves.

```text
other's own world, alone            400 settled, 330 intended
the same decision, with Eₕ in       350 settled, 280 intended
front of its coordinate
```

A world nobody decided, produced by a decision nobody changed. That is the hazard experiment 10
handed over — *an Event arriving before a non-Event coordinate moves a cut and produces a world
nobody decided* — measured for the first time.

## And the derivation that answers is not the one three results assumed

This is the finding. Experiment 09 asked for a way to say *this insertion cannot have changed what I
decided*; experiment 10 derived what a decision is **about**; and the reading three results have
carried is that recording the second answers the first. It does not.

> **An insertion is, by construction, an entry the receiving record never admitted.** So nothing in
> that record resolves against it, and no reach closure over any of its worlds can contain it. A
> derivation over what a record depends on therefore answers *harmless* for **every** insertion,
> including the one that is not.

Measured rather than argued: no entry of the receiving record's journal names either inserted
address, anywhere in what it holds. The sweep is shown able to find a reference when there is one —
the Event that settles the fund names the fund, and the same sweep sees that.

**Why the closure cannot reach it, stated as the shape rather than as the case.** What `Eₕ` changes
is `head_as_of(known_at)`, which is *the latest Event recorded no later than that instant*. The world
depends on that answer, and the answer depends on **no Event existing** between `E₀` and the instant.
A closure over entries holds what a world reaches; it cannot hold an absence. So the one thing an
insertion can violate is the one thing a dependence set is not made of.

## What answers instead

Re-derivation. Produce the world against the extended journal and compare identities — which needs
nothing the record does not hold, and is total where the closure is vacuous.

That is a smaller claim than *record what a decision depends on* and a stronger one. It also means
**nothing has to be written down**: M2 was written expecting a claim a record would make and a check
that would falsify it, and there is no claim. The record computes the answer or it does not.
