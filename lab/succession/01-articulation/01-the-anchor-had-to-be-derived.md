# Observation 1 — The anchor had to be derived, and it nearly ended the experiment

The method says the 27 non-housed claims are *placed by their kind and their anchor, mechanically*.
Building the instrument found the obvious thing nobody had checked: **the classification holds no
anchor.** A `Claim` carries a run, a text, a verdict and a standing, and nothing that says what it is
about.

So an anchor has to come from the claim's own words, and only two derivations are honest — the two
the record itself supplies. Anything else is this laboratory reading the 46 claims again and calling
the reading mechanical, which is the hazard `README.md` says this row has and the other two do not.

## The measurement that chose the rule

```text
by identity   a backticked hex prefix that uniquely prefixes an identity the record holds
                1  of the 27
by label      a whole word that is an agent label the journal admits
                9  of the 27
                    ────
anchored        9   because the one identity claim is among the nine
overflow       18   in every carving alike
```

**Identities alone would have ended it.** One claim of twenty-seven means 26 in the overflow of both
B and C, and two carvings that differ by a single claim answer nothing at all. The rule reads labels
because of that number, and the number is here rather than in the result because choosing an
instrument in the open is allowed and tuning a carving after its run is not.

Only **agent** labels count, and not the roles, resources and verbs the journal also labels. A claim
saying *spend* is not a claim about the `spend` action; it is a sentence using a word. Fixed before
anything ran.

## What the overflow is, and the protocol already called it

**Eighteen of twenty-seven attach to nothing** — and they attach to nothing *identically* in B and in
C, because neither the entity graph nor the decision list is what they are about. The protocol
anticipated the shape (*the size of the overflow is itself a result*) without anticipating the size.

It is worth saying plainly what that leaves. B and C can differ over **nine claims**, and P2 rests on
four of them. The protocol already declared P2's base thin — *four claims of four hundred and
twenty-three* — and this is a second thinness, measured, and not the same four: that one is about
**kinds**, this one is about **anchors**.

## The control, because a rule that matched noise would look like this too

The 19 housed claims are the ones a reader can already get off the files, so they are the ones most
likely to quote them. The rule reaches:

```text
14 of 19   housed
 9 of 27   not housed
```

Nearly three times the rate. The rule is reading the record rather than matching prose.

## Two things the red pass found, and one of them is a correction

**The guard caught two of my own arithmetic errors** before anything was reported: I wrote nine
resolutions where there are ten (two claims name two identities each), and twelve housed where there
are fourteen. Both were re-measured independently rather than copied out of the failure.

**And the whole-word boundary is protective, not load-bearing.** Replacing it with a plain `contains`
changes **no number** — measured, and then explained: none of the four labels ever occurs inside a
longer word in this testimony. It is kept, because the next one is not promised to be so tidy, and
it is recorded as unexercised so that nobody reads the boundary as a finding.

That is the `ver-o-teste-falhar` case that matters here: the unit test for `names()` passes a
`household` of its own and goes red on the mutation, while the real corpus never exercises the
branch. A guard green on injected input and untouched on the default path is a guard that has
measured its author's intent.

## What is not built yet

The three carvings. What exists is the record as a later reader has it, and where a claim attaches —
the two things every carving is a function of. The generator that emits A, B and C comes next, and
nothing in it may move these numbers.
