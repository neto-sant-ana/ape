# Two agents forged the party, and neither was asked to

`by` is checkable as a reference and uncheckable as an attribution. That was measured by the CLI's
coordination experiment, is stated in the crate's own docstring, and is carried into this experiment as
established rather than as a question.

What is new is who tested it. **Two of the three agents, independently and unprompted, rewrote the
field on a throwaway copy to see what the record would say** — and both got the same answer.

## What each of them did

Finance, given only an invoice and a deadline:

> In a throwaway copy of the repository: rewriting `by` to the **market** — the counterparty that
> receives — reconstructs **without refusal**, and `decided market` answers with finance's world.
> Rewriting it to a `RoleId` is refused, with the correct message.

The reader, given only a report and asked what to do next, on the premise that the Source is finance's
line:

> Change `by` from finance to market and the record rebuilds, corroborates, and then answers that
> **market** decided it. What the check does buy is only that the named party had already been
> admitted — a `by` naming a role id is refused.

Same probe, same two outcomes, arrived at from two different tasks. Neither agent was told the field
was interesting.

## What that settles about the pre-registration

**I3 is refuted as stated.** Its refutation condition was:

> *Refuted if* a reader given only the repository can partition the lineage by party without being
> told there are two.

Finance did exactly that. It was told it acts for finance and nothing else; it found the other party by
reading the journal, and its output lists the worlds each of them decided. The parties are knowledge, so
a reader that reads knowledge finds them.

**I5 is refuted too.** It predicted a claim resting on `by` might be a fourth register — neither
settleable nor placeable out of reach:

> *Refuted if* such a claim sorts cleanly once the check is run.

It sorts cleanly, and both agents ran the check. *This world is finance's* is not unfalsifiable and not
verifiable; it is **verifiable as a reference and demonstrably unbacked as an attribution**, and the
demonstration is a two-line edit anyone can repeat. A register needs a claim that resists sorting, and
this one is sorted by running the probe.

## What survives, narrowed twice

Two residues, and both were named by the agents rather than by me.

**A reader cannot ask which decisions have no party.** Finance put it plainly: the genesis names
nobody, asking for finance's worlds simply does not list it, and there is no way to ask the
complement. *Unclaimed* and *not this party's* are the same silence, which is what an optional field
costs.

**And attribution is claim-shaped in a way an output hides.** The reader's own framing is the sharpest
statement of it anywhere in this experiment:

> The premise of the whole exercise — that `791528…` is *finance's* line — is witnessed by whoever
> wrote the record and by nothing else.

That is about the *premise of its task*, not about a field. An agent handed a merge is handed an
attribution, acts on it, and cannot check it. What it can check is that the name resolves to somebody
who existed — which is a different sentence, and both agents said so unprompted rather than letting
their output read as verification.

## The one thing this adds to what was already known

Not the property. The property was measured and written down.

What it adds is that the property is **legible to a caller from the outside**. An agent with the
repository and no access to the reasoning that shaped the field reproduces, in one probe, both halves
of what the field buys and does not buy — and then declines to overclaim in its own report.

A record whose limits a reader can discover for itself is a different kind of record from one whose
limits are documented and take the documentation's word for it. That is the whole of the addition, and
it is about the record rather than about the agents.
